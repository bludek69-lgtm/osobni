💭 How I think about money

A personal view — what finances mean to me and what I make decisions based on.

How I think about finances

I take finance as long term system, not as a series of quick decisions. If I were to write the order from top to bottom, it would look something like this:

  1. Reserve — than anything else. Peace of mind before a forced sale.
  2. Regularity — monthly rhythm beats one-off events.
  3. Understanding the risk — what I have to lose and if I can stand it.
  4. Selection of tools — broader ETFs, stocks, gold, cash, crypto.
  5. Speculation — until the very end and only from the part I can afford to lose.

I add four principles to everything: patience, regularity, records a discipline. Understanding risk is more important than finding the single right stock.

💡 Concepts in brief. DCA = regular shopping regardless of price. INTENDED = patient holding (originating from misspelling "hold"). ETFs = basket of many stocks at once (e.g. "all US market"). Stock picking = manually selected individual companies.

Hardware wallets in general

What it does: keeps private keys offline.
For whom: anyone holding crypto at a value they don't want to lose.
Attention: buy only from the official manufacturer, never from a bazaar or from an unverified seller. Never take a photo of the seed, never store it in the cloud.

2FA

What it does: a second authentication factor via an authentication app or dongle.
For whom: wherever there is something to lose — bank, broker, e-mail.
Attention: avoid SMS 2FA (SIM swap attacks) if possible.

Read-only API keys

What it does: will allow an external app to read balances but not trade or withdraw.
For whom: who uses portfolio aggregators.
Attention: always check that the key does not have "withdraw" or "trade" permissions.

Advances

What it does: copies of tables, PDF statements and key documents.
For whom: anyone who records finances digitally.
Attention: at least 3 copies in 2 physically different locations (3-2-1 rule).

Checklist before buying a stock

I go through this list before sending a stock instruction. It's not a dogma — it's a lifeline against impulse buying.

  • What does the company do — do I understand the product and the market?
  • Sales and profit — growing, stagnating, falling?
  • Cash flow — does the company generate real cash?
  • Debt — how much and under what conditions?
  • Margin — operating, gross, net.
  • Valuation — P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA versus industry.
  • Competition — who is better/worse and at what?
  • Company management — does it have skin in the game?
  • Risks — what can go wrong with the thesis?
  • Reason for purchase — why now?
  • Terms of sale — write them down in advance.
  • Position in the portfolio — how much weight will you gain?

Checklist before buying an ETF

ETFs look like one product, but the differences between them determine long-term returns. Before buying, I mainly go through the following points:

  • TER — total cost (the lower the better).
  • Domicile — where the fund "lives" (Ireland, Luxembourg and others).
  • Accumulator vs. distribution — does it reinvest or send dividends?
  • Pool size — small funds tend to have a higher risk of cancellation.
  • Replication — physical (holding shares) or synthetic (via swaps)?
  • Currency — fund currency vs. base currency vs. my currency
  • Composition — what is top-10 holdings?
  • Regions — global, US, EM, Europe?
  • Sectors — IT, healthcare, energy, finance?
  • Overlay — I don't already have a similar ETF?
  • Liquidity — how much is traded daily?
  • Purpose in the portfolio — why do I want him there?

Crypto without rose-colored glasses

⚠️ Important: crypto is a high risk segment. Many projects may end at zero. It's not enough to watch the price — without security and an understanding of tokenomics, it's roulette. Invest only the part you can afford to lose.

Checklist before buying a token

  • Market cap and ranking
  • VAT (fully diluted valuation) — value at full issuance
  • Liquidity and daily trading volume
  • Tokenomics — emissions, inflation, allocation
  • Unlocks — when large packages are released for the team/VC
  • Real use — or just a marketing pitch?
  • Team — public, anonymous, doxxed?
  • Community and development activity (commits, PRs)
  • Exchanges — how many centralized is the listing?
  • Smart contract audit, bug bounty
  • Scam risk — rug pull, honeypot, copy-paste fork
  • Long-term meaning — does this thesis hold up in 5 years?
  • Safe deposit — exchange, hot wallet, hardware wallet?

Layers of crypto risk

Core assets

Large, long-running networks with history. Risky, but with the least chance of disappearing overnight.

Risk altcoins

Smaller cap, higher volatility, projects that still prove that they will last on the market.

Speculation

Microcaps, memecoins, news. More of a lottery than an investment — if at all, on a small scale.

Better to avoid

Guaranteed returns, "send me BTC and I'll send you double" schemes, unaudited staking contracts.

Investor safety minimum

In addition to market risk, I also deal with the protection of access and documents. My minimum checklist:

  • A strong, unique password for each service
  • Password Manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePassXC)
  • 2FA application (Authenticator), ideally also a hardware key
  • Avoid SMS 2FA if possible — SIM swapping is a real threat
  • Hardware wallet for larger crypto amounts
  • Never take pictures of the seed with your phone
  • Never store seed in the cloud (Google Drive, iCloud, Dropbox)
  • API keys for brokers and exchanges only read-only
  • Beware of phishing — always check the URL, not the logo in the email
  • Do not sign unknown on-chain transactions
  • Never believe in "guaranteed returns" and time pressure
  • Backup of important documents 3-2-1 (3 copies, 2 media, 1 outside the house)

Monthly investment routine

Monthly rhythm beats one-off events. My routine might look like this (without specific amounts):

  • Download exports from brokers and exchanges
  • Load and review transactions in custom applications — PT and Ledger
  • Update prices in the portfolio
  • Check the distribution of asset classes
  • Check the cash reserve
  • Store the documents in the annual folder
  • Don't deal with the daily noise of the market
  • Rebalance once a quarter / half a year

Annual audit

Once a year I sit down and calmly review how the portfolio actually looked. No corrections in affect — just going through the checklist:

  • Real portfolio return (net, after fees and taxes)
  • Risk — the biggest drop of the year, concentration of positions
  • Fees — broker, exchange, ETF TER, currency conversions
  • Tax returns and documents
  • Changing goals — what has changed in a year?
  • Change in income or expenses — does the reserve still make sense?
  • Rebalancing to target layout
  • Inspection of brokers — insurance, regulation, conditions
  • Review of crypto wallets — firmware, seed, backups
  • Backup of documents — taxes are asked back years ago
