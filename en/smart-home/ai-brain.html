🏠 Experience · AI Brain

Checking the running of the household

Brain Guardian helps check if the household is behaving as expected. It looks for discrepancies in states and logs to make it easier to find the problem and prepare a fix.

Technical details

A smart home that controls itself. Detects ghost values, verifies expected state, blocks broken deployments, analyzes logs daily via Gemini.

LIVE sh_brain_guardian_v1

Brain Guardian

Top-level supervisor. Aggregates the outputs of the validator and expected state engine, calculates health score (currently 92/100). In the event of a critical drop, it will sound an alarm.

LIVE sh_expected_state_engine_v1

Expected State Engine

For each time + mode calculates what it should be on / off. It serves as a reference against the real state — the basis for anomaly detection.

LIVE sh_state_validator_v1

State Validator

It compares the expected and real state. Detects ghost values ​​(variables that lie) and stuck flags. First production detection: priority_active=tts ghost after 00:40.

LIVE sh_release_guard_v1

Release Guard

Runs canary checks (3 test calls) before deploying a new version of the script. If the Brain Guardian score drops, the release is blocked and the old script remains.

LIVE sh_self_healing_v4

Self-Healing Engine

4 detectors every 10 minutes: orphan requests, stuck events, audio deadlocks, offline critical devices. Auto-reset without my intervention.

LIVE sh_gemini_diagnostic_v1

Gemini Diagnostics

Every day at 11:40. It reads JSONL logs from SSD on Raspberry Pi (from 27.5.2026 — previously Google Sheets), sends Gemini Flash with a diagnostic prompt, saves the result (priority + fix suggestion) to the dashboard on the AI ​​page.

LIVE (May 2) sh_intent_engine_v1

Intent Engine

Every minute he asks: "What is the house trying to do?" The answer is one of 8 intentions (peaceful sleep, night trip, morning wake-up, arrival, departure, ...) plus a score of 0-100 how confident the system is. It serves as a higher layer above the individual scripts — one comprehensive picture of the situation instead of dozens of fragmented signals.

LIVE (May 2) sh_health_monitor_v1

Health Monitor

Every 5 minutes it checks 5 health indicators — boiler, audio pipeline, presence detection, sleep state machine, energy loggers. When someone starts doing strange things, a push notification will come to the phone with a specific description (anti-flap guarded so as not to spam the user during short blips).

LIVE (May 2) sh_kill_switch

Kill Switch — standby switch

The only variable yes/no. When you switch to yes, 6 key scripts (decision brain, prediction, morning routine, patrol, heating) will stop at the beginning and do nothing the next time it starts. The way to ensure that the human operator always has the last word. Reset with one click.

LIVE (May 14) sh_room_vacancy_guardian_v1 v1.1

Vacancy Guardian — heartbeat detection

Makes sure the bathroom doesn't have lights on or the radio playing long after someone has left. Version 1.1 (5/14) added two timestamps: heartbeat (the script started running) a last_run (the script ran out of time). When the first one is fresh but the second one is old, we see exactly: cron fires, but the script is killed in the middle due to memory pressure. Plus stuck-state reset — when the bathroom is empty and the audio mode keeps saying radio_bathroom, will reset it to idle so the radio will never return from an empty bathroom by itself.

Philosophy · How to talk to AI

Why people don't trust AI

A simple motto: a stupid question, a stupid answer.

I often hear that AI doesn't work because it writes nonsense. But in most cases, a simple rule applies: a stupid question will get a stupid answer.

AI is not a magic oracle. It's a tool. If you ask her an imprecise question, don't give her a context, don't state a goal, and don't tell her exactly what is expected of her, the result tends to be weak. The AI then just guesses, fills in the blanks, and sometimes produces an answer that looks smart but is practically useless.

But when a person learns to ask specific questions, add examples, constraints, resources and clearly describe the desired output, AI turns from a toy into a very powerful helper. It's not that the AI doesn't work. Often it's more about the fact that we don't know how to talk to her properly.

An example of the difference

Bad question: Write me something about AI.

A better question: Write a short, easy-to-understand text for laymen about why the AI sometimes gives wrong answers and explain that the quality of the answer depends on the quality of the input.

That's why I always tell people: AI is not a substitute for thinking. It's a thought enhancer. When we give her chaos, she gives us chaos back. If we give her a clear assignment, she can help us significantly.
