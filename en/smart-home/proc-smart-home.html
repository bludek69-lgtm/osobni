📖 Pillar · Story

Why at all smart home

Smart home is not about toys. It is management of energy, comfort and stability. This page explains why it makes sense — from practice, not advertising. It is based on the real SMART HOME OS architecture, not on marketing promises.

Introduction

Three reasons why I do it

When people hear smart home, imagine automatic curtains and Alexa playing music on command. But that's it 2% of what it's really about. The other 98% deal with three real things that you don't deal with every day — because you don't notice them until they're gone:

  • Energy — lights that shine unnecessarily. A boiler that heats regardless of who is at home. Appliances I forgot about.
  • Comfort — bathroom warm when I go in it. A radio that turns on in the shower and turns off when I leave. Dim light at night so I don't go blind on the way to the toilet.
  • Stability — a system that survives a single script crash, sensor failure, or user error and doesn't start doing stupid things.

This page focuses mainly on the first point — energy. Because it is the most visible practical benefit and the entire rest of philosophy can be explained on it.

Reality without automation

Where the most energy is wasted

Classic apartment. No automation. What happens even when no one notices.

💡 The lights shine unnecessarily

You go into the kitchen, turn on the light, eat and leave. The light stays on until someone notices. Sometimes hours.

🌡 Heating runs regardless of presence

The thermostat has a fixed schedule — it heats to 21°C all afternoon, even when you're at work. You come home and still have to wait 30 minutes for it to heat up because it shut off at 4pm.

🔌 Device in standby

TV, set-top box, game console, monitor, chargers — even in the "off" state they draw 5-15 W each. 24 hours a day. That adds up to kilowatt-hours a year that no one notices on the bill, but they're there.

⏰ Bad timing

The washing machine turns on when it has a price peak. The boiler heats at noon, when the rate is the most expensive. The boiler switches on at 5 in the morning, even though I don't get up until 7. Little things that add up to make a big difference.

None of these things not a disaster. Each of them costs a few crowns a day. But all together, 365 days a year, are the reason why an ordinary household has an unnecessarily higher energy bill by tens of percent.

Practice

How SMART HOME OS handles each of those things

A specific pipeline. What actually runs, not what is in the picture.

💡 Lights — automation according to space and daylight

  • Shutdown on departure — when I open the door to leave and there is no motion outside, the lights turn off after a short timeout. No need to remember.
  • Lux-driven activation — the window lux sensor measures daylight. Below the threshold (typically 50 lx), the lights turn on. Above the threshold, they won't turn on — even if the movement worked — because there's enough sun.
  • Damping instead of full power — evening 0.4 dim warm temperature. Morning 0.4 gentle. At 0.85. There's no hard "on/off" — it's there contextual intensity.
  • Privacy guard when blinds are open — if the blind is up and I'm on the toilet, the lights in the open space go out. So that the world won't see where I am now. A subtle but real feature.

🌡 Heating — prediction, not reaction

  • Per-zone TRV — Dining room, Bathroom, Toilet each has its own schedule. No "central 21 °C whole apartment".
  • 3 modes — auto / manual / off. The car has 4 daily phases (morning/day/evening/night) with a different target temperature in each.
  • Anti-cycling guard — the boiler does not switch on earlier than 3 minutes after switching off, even if a demand arises. Boiler life vs. temperature accuracy.
  • Pre-wake boost — the bathroom heats up to 23 °C 15 minutes BEFORE the alarm. When I get up, it's warm. No waiting.
  • Weather adjust — below -5 °C outside, the target temperature rises by +1 °C. Above +15 °C it drops by -0.5 °C. Reactively based on real weather, not just the day of the week.
  • Occupancy bonus — when motion in the zone < 10 min, +0.5 °C. No one is home, only the foundation is holding. It's not about thermostat-ON, it's about occupancy calibration.

🔌 Device — switching off standby and monitoring consumption

  • Plug-level metering — Oven, Microwave, Refrigerator, TV, Washing Machine, Boiler measure watts real-time. I can see when what really pulls.
  • Standby cut to exit — TV, monitor, sound bar plug are turned off in "away" mode. No idle 8W all day.
  • Washing machine done detection — measurement of power drop → state "done". I don't have to wait or go to check. Phone notifications.
  • Overheat protection — kitchen appliances have a guard script. When power > threshold (e.g. oven switched on at 220 °C without user attention), notification.
  • PC plug auto-off — when the laptop disconnects Wi-Fi (= I left for work), the socket near the monitor turns off after 10 minutes. No unnecessary switching on.

🔊 Audio and others — turning off when it no longer makes sense

  • Sleep guard — when sleep mode comes, the audio turns off immediately. No matter what he plays.
  • Bathroom radio auto-stop — turns off 5 minutes after the last motion in the bathroom. It's not sobbing, it's not after hours.
  • Giuseppe vacuum cleaner — is triggered when no one is home. Never at night, never during a visit.
  • Fyrtur roller blind — privacy at night, daylight during the day. Saves the climate (cools in the summer by drawing in the sun).

Brain on brain

The role of AI in the system

AI is not here for AI's sake. It's here because some things can't be handled well by rigid rules.

Gradual learning

The system remembers patterns. What time do I typically get up? When do I go to sleep When do I get back from work? No training, simply a picture is gradually built up from the events.

Gemini diagnostics

When the system reports something (anomaly, drift, error), Gemini reads the context and suggests what to do with it. He doesn't do the action himself — he's an advisor, not an agent.

Brain Guardian

A layer that monitors other automata. Defines "expected state" and compares with reality. When something drifts, he says. He detected real bugs before I did.

Behavioral response

Instead of "turn on the heating at 6:30 in the morning" there is "when there are 15 minutes until the alarm AND the user is not still awake AND the bathroom is below the target temperature → boost". Rules have context.

What does it actually do?

Real impact

There are no exact figures in percentages. Measuring in a real apartment is difficult — the weather changes consumption by 50% from year to year, energy prices change, the rhythm of life changes. Instead of numbers, I will describe what actually stopped causing the problem.

  • Reduction of unnecessary lighting — the kitchen light is no longer on for 4 hours when I'm in the bathroom. Open space lamps turn themselves off when I leave. Lux sensor blocks activation in broad daylight.
  • More efficient heating — the bathroom only heats up when there is a reason to do so (pre-wake boost, motion, planned hot water). Per-zone preset instead of flat rate.
  • Elimination of "forgotten devices" — TV, monitor, sound bar plug will be switched off on "away". The washing machine notifies me that it is done instead of staying in the finishing phase for 8 hours.
  • Less manual intervention — less time wondering if I turned something off. Fewer decisions every day. Comfort.
  • Stable behavior — automatics that do not overlap. No "2 flows fighting over the same bulb". The priority engine arbitrates conflicts.

The actual numbers — if there ever are — will be posted in the Build Log section, not here. This page describes principles, not marketing bullet points.

Summary

Smart home = energy management + comfort + control

It's not about gadgets. It's not about the app. It's not about me being able to light up with my voice. It's about that I live in a house that remembers who I am, what I do and what I need — a energy does one of those things, the second comfort, the third stability.

When done right, you don't even notice it. Only when you turn it off will you find out how much work the system has been doing for you.

🤖 Bonus box

What the system does automatically — without your input

  • 💡 Turns off the lights — when leaving, when there is enough daylight, when in sleep mode
  • 🌡 Optimizes heating — pre-wake boost, occupancy adjust, weather adjust, per-zone TRV
  • 🔌 Monitors consumption — standby cut to away, plug-level metering, overheat protection
  • 👁 Responds to presence — multi-signal arrival/departure, motion+presence guards, sleep state machine
  • 🩺 He diagnoses himself — Brain Guardian validator, anomaly detection, self-healing watchdog
  • 🔇 Turns off the audio — sleep guard, bathroom radio auto-stop, away-cut

None of these points are theories. All are implemented, deployed on Homey Pro 2026 and run 24/7 in a real apartment. Details of each pipeline can be found in AI Brain, Heating, Lights and other pages.
