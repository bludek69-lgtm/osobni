Montepulciano: Renaissance, Cellars and Clock Keepers

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20262 minutes of reading
Video · 4:55 · YouTube loads after launchOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources and materials →

Montepulciano has several faces in the video: the church below the town, the rising streets, the wine cellars and the tiny figure above the clock. It is the combination of great architecture with a small, slightly mysterious detail that makes the walk more than just a visit to another Tuscan hilltop town.

A city between two landscapes

Visit Tuscany places Montepulciano on the ridge separating the Val d'Orcia from the Valdichiana. It resembles the medieval layout of the city and distinctive Renaissance architecture. When visiting, it is therefore interesting to observe the individual houses and the rise itself: the street gradually changes height and opens up different views outside.

Madonna di San Biagio represents a different type of stop than denser urban areas. According to the regional portal, it is a Renaissance work by Antonio da Sangallo the Elder under the city walls. In relation to the following streets, it acts as an independent architectural unit, which benefits from distance and time to look around.

Cellars as part of the site

The winemaking tradition belongs to the image of Montepulciano as well as the Renaissance facade. The video brings it closer by visiting the cellars. For your own trip, it is good to distinguish between a free city walk and a specific visit to a winery: confirm openings, tours and any reservations with the given operation.

Underground and on the street, light, sound and scale change. It is this contrast that can be more interesting to watch than a quick list of familiar names. For a while, the city on the hill becomes a place of work, storage and trading, as well as a view of the Tuscan countryside.

Who watches the clock

Pulcinella, a figure primarily associated with Naples, appears on the tower. Italia.it describes a wooden statue covered in sheet metal that announces the hour by striking a bell. Why this motif is found in Montepulciano remains the subject of various interpretations. The story of the priest from Campania is a local legend, not a clearly documented explanation.

It's a small thing that makes sense to raise your head. After the monumental church and the wine cellars, it shows a different form of the city's memory: the daily sound of the clock and an unusual figure that the visitor can remember as well as the entire square.

For a walk, leave time and strength for the rising streets. If you follow up with a trip to Val d'Orcia, you will see the same relationship between the city and the landscape from the opposite side. The next city stop can be Siena.
