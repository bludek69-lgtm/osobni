Volcanoes of the Bay of Naples: from Vesuvius to the islands

Vesuvius is the best-known outline of the Bay of Naples, but the volcanic story continues far beyond its crater. The film combines my own footage from Vesuvius and Pompeii with photographs of other places and historical context.

Vesuvius and two buried cities

The opening explores Somma and Vesuvius, the view into the crater and the record preserved in old photographs. It then moves through Pompeii’s streets and public spaces into homes and traces of everyday life. Herculaneum offers another perspective on a city affected by the eruption.

My short personal film of the climb remains in the Vesuvius article. This new documentary adds a wider view of the whole bay.

The Phlegraean Fields: a landscape with many centres

From 6:50, the film moves through Solfatara and Pisciarelli to Astroni, Pozzuoli, Monte Nuovo and Lake Averno. Baiae, Miseno and underwater scenes complete the picture. The places are connected by the origin of a landscape now shared by towns, roads and everyday life.

Separate chapters introduce scientific monitoring and preparations for emergencies. Conditions can change: use the official institutional links in the film’s sources when planning a visit.

Ischia, Procida, Vivara and Nisida

The final part turns to the islands, moving from Ischia to Procida and Vivara and ending at Nisida and the crater bay of Porto Paone. The short preview below focuses on Nisida.

Using the film to plan a trip

The stops are not a one-day itinerary or a promise of unrestricted access. Check entry conditions, bookings and restrictions before travelling. Continue with the Campania guide and my original footage from Pompeii and Naples.
