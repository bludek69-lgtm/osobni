Hoslovický mill: how people lived and worked by the water

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20265 minutes of reading

Thatched roofs, yard and water above the building. The Hoslovický mill in Strakonicko tells of a time when home, economy and work were one whole. In the first part of Journeys with a story, we watch how the power of water turned into flour and how the life of several generations took place around the mill.

The video accompanies the place through photographs. This article adds context and practical links to it for your own trip.

First water, then flour

Proximity to the stream alone was not enough for the mill to function. The water needed to be brought to where it could transfer its energy to the wheel. In Hoslovice, this path consists of a drive, a pond above the building and a wooden trough called vantroky. The municipal description mentions a drive approximately 150 meters long.

The individual parts are worth watching together. The pond helped with the supply of water, the insiders led it to the wheel, and the motion was transmitted to the mill via shafts and gears. There, the grain from the hopper reached the grinding stones. What looks like a set of wooden parts in the photo is a well-thought-out path from natural energy to food.

At the same time, the miller was not a mere spectator of the spinning wheel. He had to know the equipment, monitor the grinding and take care of the waterway. To look at just the bike would be to see one part of the story; its meaning becomes apparent only in connection with the surrounding landscape and the mill.

Behind the walls of the mill, the home began

The mill building combines the work and living areas. An oven and a stove were preserved in the living room, and there were other domestic and storage spaces next to it. The rooms are reminiscent of a time when cooking, stocking and housekeeping took place just a few steps away from the grind.

The yard also explains that it was about more than processing grain for the surrounding area. The homestead includes stables with a stable, a barn, an orchard and meadows. Life was governed by the care of animals, crops and seasons. Behind the barn, the construction of the gentour was preserved: a device that used animal traction to power the work.

During the tour, you can notice little things that easily fit into the big story: where the supplies were stored, how the rooms connected to each other, or how close the residential and farm buildings were to each other. It is these connections that help to understand the daily running of the homestead.

What does the year 1352 mean

The administrator of the mill mentions the first written mention as early as 1352. However, this does not mean that all the current buildings were built in the 14th century. Historical buildings were gradually repaired and transformed.

A more concrete view is offered by the dating of the wood of the granary ceiling: according to municipal documents, it comes from trees felled in 1568–1569. Another trace is a gneiss gneiss from 1654, which shows a mill with one wheel and farm. So each source tells about a different part of the past; together they show the long duration of the place.

The Harants and the preserved environment

The Harant family bought the mill in 1829. The story of the last owners is one of the most important chapters in its history. The museum describes their isolation from society since the 1950s in connection with the persecution of the communist regime.

The preserved old equipment is therefore not just a romantic backdrop. It is also based on the demanding conditions of the people who actually lived here. When looking at the world and the economy, it is good to perceive, in addition to the value of preserved things, the work and limitations that life at that time brought.

After the death of the last owner, the area was saved. The South Bohemian Region bought it in 2005 and handed it over to the administration of the Museum of Central Pootaví Strakonice. After restoration, the mill opened to the public in April 2008; in the same year it received the status of a national cultural monument. Today's visit is the result of preserving the original environment and subsequent care.

How to plan your visit

The mill is located in the village of Hoslovice in Šumava Podlesí, approximately 17 kilometers southwest of Strakonice. Use for orientation map to video. The address of the premises is Hoslovice 36, 387 19 Particles.

  • Deadline: the normal visitor season is from April to October. Outside of it, check specific events and visit options with the administrator.
  • Device operation: demonstrations of the operation of the bike, crafts and baking are tied to the program. They cannot be promised at every visit.
  • Accessibility: the historic mill is not barrier-free. If you have limited mobility, make arrangements with the administrator before the trip.
  • Current conditions: check the opening hours, entrance fees and visiting rules on the official website.

Practical information verified on 16 November 2026. Official website of the mill ↗

When you go to the place, try to look at it as a connected whole: from the water supply to the wheel, from the mill to the chamber and from there to the yard. There is a story hidden in these contexts, which makes it worth stopping by Hoslovice.
