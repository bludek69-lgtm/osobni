Spain: seven places between rocks and history

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20263 minutes of reading
Video · 6:21 · YouTube loads on startupOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources and materials →Short demo ↗

Houses under a rock overhang, a landscape marked by Roman mining, an unusual river and medieval towns. The Spanish part of Hidden Gems selects seven places where it is easy to see how different stories one country can offer.

Setenil de las Bodegas, Las Médulas, Río Tinto, Albarracín, Frías, Bardenas Reales and Besalú do not lie in one small neighborhood. The video connects them with the theme of discovery, not the promise of a fast route across Spain. For your own trip, it is useful to choose one or two places according to the region in which you will be staying.

Setenil: when the rock is part of the mansion

In Setenil de las Bodegas, a theme that returns in other travel stories begins: one does not build in an empty space, but adapts to the place. The houses under the rock attract attention at first glance. But the scale of the street and the relationship of individual buildings to the terrain is also interesting.

When watching, you can focus on the difference between a distant view of the village and the space you actually walk through. The same rock acts once as a landscape feature and secondly as an immediate part of the everyday environment.

Las Médulas and Río Tinto: reading the landscape differently

Las Médulas remind us that a distinctive landscape does not have to be the result of natural processes alone. According to the official tourism website of Spain it is the remains of extensive Roman gold mining. When looking at the shapes here, it is therefore useful to add the history of human work to nature.

Río Tinto offers another transformation of the view of the landscape, this time through the river and its surroundings. In addition to settlements and monuments, the video thus gives space to places where the appearance of the environment itself plays a major role. When visiting, it pays to look for visitor approaches and viewpoints, not automatically head for the shortest route to a point on the map.

Albarracín, Frías and Besalú: the city in a wider picture

These three objectives return the story to the development, streets and historic buildings. It is interesting to compare how the impression of the city changes depending on whether you look at it as a whole or at an individual passage and facade. Bridges and fortifications in such a view make sense as part of a functioning settlement.

You don't have to look for a single mandatory photo to stop yourself. Try to read the place from the arrival to the historic part through the main streets to the view back to the whole. A video can be the beginning of such an observation, not a list of exact images that need to be repeated.

Bardenas Reales and pace selection

The Bardenas Reales in Navarre complement the cities with a semi-desert landscape shaped by water and wind. Spain.info draws attention to different parts of the territory and their different environments. One shot does not have to describe the whole park.

You will open all seven objectives in the map to the video. For a specific day, choose a smaller area and check the visitor rules and the condition of the access roads. In the case of large natural sites, the time for a tour cannot be derived only from the time it takes to reach them. Leave space for the landscape itself between individual goals.
