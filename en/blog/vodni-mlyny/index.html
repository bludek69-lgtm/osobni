Six watermills: life and work by the mill wheel

Luděk · Cestovatel6922. 11. 20263 minutes of reading
Video · 6:00 · YouTube loads after launchOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources →

A mill was not just a machine for making flour. It was a house, a workshop and a home for a whole family. Six Czech and Moravian mills show how water turned the wheels, how the movement reached the millstone and how close work and everyday life were.

Slup: a large mill with four wheels

Judging by its façade, you would expect a château in Slup, yet grain was milled under its Renaissance gables. A mill race brings the water and four wheels stand side by side along the long wall. The Technical Museum in Brno presents four ways of processing grain here. After milling, the grain still had to be sifted and sorted. Slup from 0:00 ↗

Walsch’s and Rosenauer’s mills

Walsch’s mill in Týniště near Zubrnice took its present form in a rebuilding in 1803; the year is carved into a post of the mill room. Water fell on the wheel from above and turned it by its weight. The mill was also a home: a cradle hung by the tiled stove, and work and sleep were only a few steps apart. Walsch’s mill from 1:36 ↗

Rosenauer’s mill on an island opposite Horažďovice stands where handmade paper was once produced; it was converted into a grain mill in 1834. The exhibition shows weights, scales, large pulleys and a turbine. Locals told the legend of a butcher who carried a calf on his shoulders – and found he was carrying a water goblin. Rosenauer’s mill from 2:26 ↗

Mills in open-air museums

The storeyed mill from Běleč nad Orlicí now stands in Krňovice. The original house collapsed, and craftsmen rebuilt it, including its carved gallery, from measurements, older documentation and surviving parts between 2009 and 2016. In the Mill Valley of Rožnov pod Radhoštěm stands a reconstruction of Tomek’s mill from Velké Karlovice: grain fell from the hopper between two stones and the upper runner stone was turned by a vertical shaft. Krňovice from 3:30 ↗

At Veselý Kopec stands the mill from Oldřetice, moved from near Skuteč to replace an older mill that burned down in 1909. It also helped to make groats, and a plum-jam boiling house from Klešice was added to it. Millers in the Vysočina region are said to have thrown a handful of flour into the race before the first milling after winter. Veselý Kopec from 5:09 ↗

Before you visit

All six places are on the map for the video. Check opening hours and seasonal operation with each mill’s operator.
