Val d'Orcia and Crete Senesi: the landscape behind a Tuscan postcard

Photos from my archive, a view from above and context for your own journey.

Budinský Luděk16. 11. 20263 minutes of reading

A cypress alley leads to the farm, surrounded by alternating fields and rolling hills. Two photos from my archive show the Val d'Orcia on July 8, 2023. The video adds an aerial view to the article, connecting this area with the neighboring Crete Senesi landscape.

Behind the view is the work of people

Val d'Orcia is a cultural landscape. UNESCO emphasizes its relationship to Siena and the Renaissance idea of ​​an orderly countryside. The value of the place includes fields, mansions, estates and historical roads. When looking at the local hills, it therefore makes sense to also observe how people moved and cultivated them.

Simple elements are repeated in the landscape: a road, a row of trees, a building and a field. They can run quickly from the car. In a photo or video, it's time to notice their mutual arrangement.

Val d'Orcia and Crete Senesi

Both areas have their own character. The video connects the alley at Poggio Covili in Val d'Orcia with the golden hills of Crete Senesi. It is not about one common view: when planning a trip, individual places need to be distinguished.

Visit Tuscany describes the area around Asciana as a clay hill with roads and trails. Erosion is also reflected in the shapes of the slopes. View field boundaries, the rhythm of trees and individual ridges. Their form changes according to the light, vegetation and harvest season.

Choose your own stop

Start with a smaller area of maps to video. You can complement a stop in the countryside with a walk Montepulcian or continue the story Tuscany in all senses.

Before stopping at the alley

  • Distinguish between a public road and a private entrance to the farm. An aerial shot does not confirm free entry to the property.
  • For a walking tour, use the described local routes. Do not enter the sown field for the sake of photography.
  • Stop safely out of traffic. The photogenic alley itself is not a parking lot.

On the spot, take time to look beyond the most famous photo shoot. The direction of the road, the shade of trees or the border of two fields can say more about the landscape than the next item on the list of stops.
