Trôo and Rochemenier: life hidden in the rock

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20263 minutes of reading
Video · 6:09 · YouTube loads after launchOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources and materials →

The house does not have to stand in front of a rock. It can be hiding right inside it. The French Trôo and Rochemenier show two forms of housing, in which architecture was also created by taking away material. The video cuts from the village on the hillside to the yards and farms below the level of the surrounding land.

The common theme is simple: how people used rock for everyday life. The result is not only unusual facades. The arrangement of approaches, living spaces and places for work, which may not be visible from the outside at first glance, is interesting.

Trôo: the village you read on the heights

The first part of the video belongs to Trôo above the Loir valley. It is good to note the name of the river: it is the Loir, not the similarly named Loire. The main theme of this part is the mansion spread out on the slope and the connection of houses, paths and rock.

While watching, you can imagine a different way of walking through the village than on one main street. The relationships between the individual levels are as important here as the individual buildings. The door in the rock is not only an interesting detail, but an entrance to the space with its own use. Trôo in the video from 0:25 ↗

Rochemenier: courtyard open to the sky, rooms in the rock

In the second part, the arrangement changes. In Rochemenier, the visitor center preserves the former dwellings and farms excavated into the local rock called falun. An important part is the open courtyards and the rooms arranged around them.

According to official website of the museum underground chapels, stables and other parts of rural life are also presented here. Such a composition helps to imagine a functioning economy. The living room is only one part of it; around her it was necessary to store things, work and take care of animals. Rochemenier from 3:06 ↗

Two forms of the same question

Trôo and Rochemenier are good to watch together precisely because they are not identical. In the former, the central motif is the slope above the valley, in the latter, the descent into the area of ​​the yard and former farms. When comparing, you can see where the light is coming from and how a person transitions between the outdoor and indoor environments.

Rock dwellings will then cease to act as curiosities. Behind the unusual appearance, familiar needs appear: to live, to work, to have space for supplies and to connect the household with the surroundings. It is this connection to ordinary life that is most interesting about the pair of stops.

Before your own visit

You can find both places in the map to the video. Plan them as separate stops with time to move. At Trôo, distinguish between public roads, visitor facilities and private dwellings; check the operation of the museum at Rochemenier.

If you need barrier-free access, find out the specific conditions in advance. The general idea of ​​a "village in a rock" does not say anything certain about the stairs, the surface or the shape of the individual entrances. It publishes current dates and visitor options Rochemenier Museum. The goal of the trip may not be to go through as many rooms as possible; it can be an understanding of how a home was formed in the rock.
