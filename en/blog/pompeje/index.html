Pompeii: the city behind the story of the volcanic disaster

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20262 minutes of reading
Video · 7:35 · YouTube loads on startupOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources and materials →

Pompeii is best known for its ending. When walking through their streets, what stands out the most is what preceded the end: work, rest, performances, bathing and daily movement through the city. The video goes from theaters and gladiator courtyards to houses and baths to the forum.

First the city, then the disaster

The eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79 buried Pompeii and other nearby settlements. UNESCO emphasizes the extraordinary statement of these sites about the Roman environment. It is important for the visitor that he does not only look at individual rare objects: he can follow the links between the street, public building and private space.

In the video, try to compare the width of the passages, the continuity of the rooms and the way in which the more closed parts come to the open space. The city thus acquires an understandable rhythm. The forum is then not just another stop, but a place related to the journey we have already passed before.

What ordinary functions tell

Theaters, spas and houses help to imagine different forms of communal and private life. At each stop, a simple question can be asked: why did the person come here and how did they use the space? Such a view can be more useful than trying to remember many names in one trip.

With casts of victims, the tone of the visit changes. Next to urban planning and crafts, a specific human loss stands out. It is appropriate to pay respect to these places and distinguish between an explanation of the archaeological method and a mere spectacle. There is no need to rush to the next photo spot.

Walk at your own time

The video is less than eight minutes long, but the visit to the site takes place on a completely different scale. Choose a few parts that you want to get to know in a concentrated manner, and plan to move between them. The accessibility of individual houses or sectors may change; map and current status of verifications on the website of the archaeological park.

  • Before you go, check the entrances, the reservation and the currently accessible parts of the area.
  • Adapt the route to the conditions of the day and the time you have. The record is not proof of barrier-free access to all places.
  • For a wider context, watch also climbing Vesuvius a a walk through Naples.

The strongest impression can be left from a fairly simple passage down the street. A great historical event will be connected there with the idea of ​​an ordinary city and its inhabitants.
