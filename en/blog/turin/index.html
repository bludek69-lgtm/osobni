Turin: from the city salon to the Royal Palace

Luděk · Cestovatel6916. 11. 20262 minutes of reading
Video · 5:13 · YouTube loads on startupOpen a video on YouTube ↗
Places on the map →Photo sources and materials →

In this video, Turin is presented through squares, arcades and life between historic facades. The path leads from the fountains in Piazza CLN through Piazza San Carlo towards the Royal Palace. Between the large buildings, there is enough space even for the ordinary movement of people and a street parade.

A square that functions as a city room

Piazza San Carlo was nicknamed the Salon of Turin. The Italian tourist portal commemorates its opening in 1638 and the design of Carlo di Castellamonte. The regular rectangular space, archway and facade create the impression of a well-thought-out whole. In the middle stands the equestrian monument of Emanuel Filiberto, known as Caval ëd Bronze.

In the video, it is worth watching the edges of the square for a while. It is there that great architecture meets the ordinary city: walking, stopping, a cafe or passing under the arcades. The scale of the space is more apparent when the viewer has the facade and the people below at the same time.

From San Carlo to the Palace

Via Roma connects Piazza San Carlo with Piazza Castello. This gives the walk an understandable axis and, at the same time, an opportunity to compare open squares with regular street views. For orientation, use the map above the article; individual points will help differentiate places that may seem like one long urban scene at first viewing.

The footage of the Royal Palace reminds us that Turin is not just a city of cars or sports brands. Its representative center also tells about the monarch's power. However, the actual visit to the interiors needs to be planned separately: looking at the building from the outside does not tell which spaces are currently accessible.

Parade as a moment, not a permanent attraction

The street parade captured in the video changes the rhythm of the walk. For a while, the historical space becomes the place of a joint event. It is part of a particular travel record; when visiting on a different day, the same square can look calmer and sound completely different. It is differences like these that make returns to cities interesting.

  • Decide whether you mainly want a walk through the center or a visit to the museums. The second option needs its own timetable.
  • The arcade can be part of the route as well as the center of the square; try to compare both views.
  • For the current opening of the palace exhibits, use the Musei Reali website, not the older video.

If you enjoy watching how cities organize their public space, compare Turin Siena. The regular salon and the sloping Campo show two very different forms of the Italian square.
