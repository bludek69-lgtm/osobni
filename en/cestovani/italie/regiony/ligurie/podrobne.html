Liguria: what to know and how to travel

Liguria is a narrow strip between sea and mountains, connected by coastal towns and railways. The photo shows Lerici in the Golfo dei Poeti; further west lie Genoa and Cinque Terre.

On this page: Lerici and San Terenzo · Cinque Terre · Janov a Portovenere

Lerici and San Terenzo

Lerici is located on the Gulf of Golfo dei Poeti and above the harbor stands a castle. You can walk along the promenade to San Terenza and watch the boats and views across the bay along the way.

Cinque Terre

The five villages of Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola and Riomaggiore are connected by railways and a network of footpaths. Some trails may be closed or subject to fees, so check the park information before your trip.

Janov a Portovenere

Genoa offers historic streets and an old port. Portovenere, on the western side of the Golfo dei Poeti, is recognized by walking around the port towards the church of San Pietro.

Landscape and road

The narrow region between the mountains and the Ligurian Sea is made up of bays, terraced slopes and harbors. The Gulf of Golfo dei Poeti near Lerici has a different character than the densely populated surroundings of Genoa or the Cinque Terre villages.

Lerici and San Terenzo are connected by a coastal promenade. In Cinque Terre, the most practical way to get around is by train; check the condition of the trails, fees and possible closures with the park administration. When traveling by car, expect limited parking.

Local cuisine and wine

Pesto alla genovese is made from basil, olive oil, cheese, pine nuts and other ingredients; often accompanied by trofie or trenette pasta. You can also try Ligurian focaccia and chickpea farinata.

Sciacchetrà is a sweet wine associated with the Cinque Terre; the region also offers dry white wines. Due to the sloping terrain, the local vineyards are closely connected with the terracing of the landscape.

What to taste on the way

Genoese pesto combines basil, olive oil, garlic, pine nuts and cheeses. In addition to pasta, in Liguria it is also worth trying focaccia or chickpea flatbread farinata.
