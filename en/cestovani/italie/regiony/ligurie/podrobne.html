Liguria: what to know and how to travel

In Liguria, a railway, a road and a narrow strip of houses often share the space between the sea and a steep hillside.

A nearby place on the map does not necessarily mean a quick drive.

I recommend choosing one stretch of coast and exploring it by train, on foot and, when services allow, by boat.

Why choose this region?

Genoa’s port, the villages and hillside paths of Cinque Terre, and the bay around Lerici need their own time.

A few nights in one base make it easier to adapt to the weather without constant packing.

Lerici and San Terenzo

Lerici is located on the Gulf of Golfo dei Poeti and above the harbor stands a castle.

You can walk along the promenade to San Terenza and watch the boats and views across the bay along the way.

Cinque Terre

The five villages of Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola and Riomaggiore are connected by railways and a network of footpaths.

Some trails may be closed or subject to fees, so check the park information before your trip.

Janov a Portovenere

Genoa offers historic streets and an old port.

Portovenere, on the western side of the Golfo dei Poeti, is recognized by walking around the port towards the church of San Pietro.

Two detours beyond the main stops

Tellaro

Houses cluster around the small centre and waterfront church.

I would combine Tellaro with Lerici, arranging transport beforehand so the visit is not spent hunting for parking.

Noli

Look for medieval towers and the town’s relationship with the sea. Noli offers a western Ligurian stop beyond the most photographed eastern villages.

Landscape and road

The narrow region between the mountains and the Ligurian Sea is made up of bays, terraced slopes and harbors.

The Gulf of Golfo dei Poeti near Lerici has a different character than the densely populated surroundings of Genoa or the Cinque Terre villages.

Lerici and San Terenzo are connected by a coastal promenade.

In Cinque Terre, the most practical way to get around is by train; check the condition of the trails, fees and possible closures with the park administration.

When traveling by car, expect limited parking.

What to taste

Pesto alla genovese is made from basil, olive oil, cheese, pine nuts and other ingredients; often accompanied by trofie or trenette pasta.

You can also try Ligurian focaccia and chickpea farinata.

Sciacchetrà is a sweet wine associated with the Cinque Terre; the region also offers dry white wines.

Due to the sloping terrain, the local vineyards are closely connected with the terracing of the landscape.

How I would choose a meal

Genoese pesto combines basil, olive oil, garlic, pine nuts and cheeses. In addition to pasta, in Liguria it is also worth trying focaccia or chickpea flatbread farinata.

I would try pesto with local pasta and focaccia as a separate snack. Ask about nuts and cheese in prepared sauces if ingredients or allergies matter to you.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: I would choose spring or September and October for walking, guided by the forecast. After heavy rain, check the trail status even if the following morning looks fine.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Start with trains for the main coastal stops. Check seasonal boat timetables and the return journey; smaller destinations may need an additional bus or walk.
  • What to check: For Cinque Terre, check the chosen path, any fee and footwear requirements with the park. A sea view does not make a steep trail a level promenade.
