Emilia-Romagna: what to know and how to travel

Emilia-Romagna stretches from the Po and the Apennines to the Adriatic. Bologna in the photo is known for its arcades and medieval towers; other stops are Modena and Ravenna.

On this page: Bologna · Ravenna · Modena and surroundings

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Ravenna →

Bologna

The historic center is interspersed with arcades that connect Piazza Maggiore with the surrounding streets. The city is also a good place to explore the local cuisine.

Ravenna

Ravenna is known for its early Christian monuments and mosaics. The decoration can be admired, for example, in the Basilica of San Vitale; check the entry conditions before visiting.

Modena and surroundings

In Modena, the historic center and the cathedral complement the automotive tradition. Those interested can follow up on the Ferrari museums in Modena and nearby Maranello.

Landscape and road

The region stretches from the Apennines through the fertile Po Valley to the Adriatic. Bologna, Modena and Parma lie inland, while Ravenna and the seaside resorts belong to the eastern part.

The cities between Parma and Bologna can be well combined by train. Plan a special stop for Ravenna and the coast; in the Apennines, expect mountain roads and changeable weather.

Local cuisine and wine

Bologna is associated with ragù and stuffed pasta, the Parma region with Parma ham and Parmigiano Reggiano cheese. Modena is also famous for its traditional balsamic vinegar; individual designations differ in the method of production.

Lambrusco represents a group of wines often associated with the surroundings of Modena and Reggio Emilia. In Romagna you will also find wines from the Sangiovese variety.

What to taste on the way

Bologna is a good place to try tagliatelle al ragù or stuffed tortellini. Parmigiano Reggiano and balsamic vinegar have their own regions of origin; labeling on the packaging will help distinguish a traditional product from a common imitation.
