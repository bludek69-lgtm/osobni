Emilia-Romagna: what to know and how to travel

Under Bologna’s porticoes, a busy street can lead straight to a shop making fresh pasta. Emilia-Romagna also has a coast, wetlands and the Apennines.

For a first visit, I would choose a few stops along the Via Emilia and leave proper time for meals between them.

Why choose this region?

Bologna, Modena and Parma show how closely city life and food production are connected. Romagna brings its own cuisine and Adriatic landscapes.

I would avoid turning the journey into a race through famous products.

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Ravenna →

Bologna

The historic center is interspersed with arcades that connect Piazza Maggiore with the surrounding streets. The city is also a good place to explore the local cuisine.

Ravenna

Ravenna is known for its early Christian monuments and mosaics.

The decoration can be admired, for example, in the Basilica of San Vitale; check the entry conditions before visiting.

Modena and surroundings

In Modena, the historic center and the cathedral complement the automotive tradition.

Those interested can follow up on the Ferrari museums in Modena and nearby Maranello.

Two detours beyond the main stops

Brisighella

Gypsum outcrops, surrounding countryside and local olive oil justify the detour. I would build the day around a walk and one carefully chosen visit.

Comacchio

Bridges and canals introduce the lagoons and the Po Delta. Add a wetland outing after checking which routes or guided visits are currently available.

Landscape and road

The region stretches from the Apennines through the fertile Po Valley to the Adriatic.

Bologna, Modena and Parma lie inland, while Ravenna and the seaside resorts belong to the eastern part.

The cities between Parma and Bologna can be well combined by train.

Plan a special stop for Ravenna and the coast; in the Apennines, expect mountain roads and changeable weather.

What to taste

Bologna is associated with ragù and stuffed pasta, the Parma region with Parma ham and Parmigiano Reggiano cheese.

Modena is also famous for its traditional balsamic vinegar; individual designations differ in the method of production.

Lambrusco represents a group of wines often associated with the surroundings of Modena and Reggio Emilia.

In Romagna you will also find wines from the Sangiovese variety.

How I would choose a meal

Bologna is a good place to try tagliatelle al ragù or stuffed tortellini.

Parmigiano Reggiano and balsamic vinegar have their own regions of origin; labeling on the packaging will help distinguish a traditional product from a common imitation.

Tortellini in broth, tagliatelle al ragù and piadina belong to different local traditions. I would order accordingly and give the daily menu a chance.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: I would choose April to June or September and October for towns and countryside. In summer, plan beach days differently from walks through hot city centres.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Start with trains between the main cities. For a cheese producer or winery, check its exact address and the last leg from the station; it may be well outside town.
  • What to check: Book production visits in advance and confirm the language and what is included. For balsamic vinegar, compare the exact designation rather than the bottle’s shape.
