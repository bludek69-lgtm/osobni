Piedmont

Turin’s porticoes have a different rhythm from a drive along the vineyard ridges of the Langhe. The Alps remain close to both sides of Piedmont.

I recommend combining the city with one wine-growing or lake area, and planning the high mountains separately.

Examples of places: Torino · Langhe.

Why choose this region?

Piedmont is more than one wine or museum. Turin suits walking and trams, the countryside invites short stops, and lakes offer boat journeys.

Choose which landscape you want outside your door before booking a base.

In addition to Turin, Alba and the vineyards around Barolo are also worth exploring. Mountains and gastronomy can be combined here in one trip.

What to see

In Turin you can combine squares and museums; Alba is the starting point for the Langhe hills.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: I would consider May, June or September and October for towns and vineyards. An autumn visit does not guarantee truffles or availability at a tasting: arrange both ahead.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Plan Turin without a car first. For wine villages, compare arranged transfers or local tours, or appoint a driver who will not take part in tastings.
  • What to check: Keep lake and mountain days flexible around the weather. Check boats and route conditions with the operator rather than relying on an all-season photograph.

What to taste

In Piedmont, you will come across tajarin pasta, bagna càuda sauce or dishes with hazelnuts and truffles.

Truffles are available seasonally and prices vary significantly.

Barolo and Barbaresco are wines from the Nebbiolo variety in the Langhe region. Barbera and Dolcetto varieties are also known, often in a different style.
