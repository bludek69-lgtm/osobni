Molise: what to see and how to travel

The small region of Molise includes inland hills as well as a short Adriatic coast. The picture shows the old town of Termoli above the sea; the administrative center of the region is Campobasso.

On this page: Thermos · Campobasso · Mountains and villages

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Campobasso →

Thermos

The old town on the coastal promontory consists of narrow streets, a cathedral and a fortress above the sea. Termoli is also the port from which, depending on the season, connections to the Tremiti Islands depart.

Campobasso

The inland capital is located on the hillside below Monforte Castle. Historic streets and viewpoints provide a different stop than the Adriatic coast.

Mountains and villages

Molise passes from the sea to the hilly interior. Smaller villages and mountain roads are suitable for a slower journey by car, during which it is good to expect longer journeys.

Landscape and road

The small region between the Apennines and the Adriatic includes a mountainous interior, villages and a short coast near Termoli. Unlike just looking at the map, moving in the mountains can take more time.

I consider the coastal Termoli and the inland towns of Campobasso and Isernia as separate stops. When planning public transport, check connections in advance; in smaller municipalities the intervals may not be frequent.

Local cuisine and wine

Local pastas include cavatelli; in different regions they are served with sauces according to the season. Cheese, olive oil and legume dishes are also typical.

Tintilia is a variety associated with Molise. When buying, orientate yourself according to the designation of origin and the specific winemaker.

What to taste on the way

Cavatelli are small shaped pasta also widespread in Molise. They can be served with various sauces or simply with breadcrumbs; the photo recipe is one of the possible modifications.
