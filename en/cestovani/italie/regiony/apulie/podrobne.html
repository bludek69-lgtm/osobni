Apulia

Apulia forms the southeastern part of the Italian peninsula and has Adriatic and Ionian coasts. The photograph shows Alberobello and its stone trulli houses.

Bari

Bari Vecchia has narrow streets, the Basilica of Saint Nicholas and a waterfront. Leave time to explore on foot and discover local food.

Alberobello and Valle d’Itria

Alberobello is known for the conical stone roofs of its trulli. The surrounding Itria Valley also includes the towns of Locorotondo and Martina Franca.

Lecce and Salento

Lecce’s historic centre is known for its ornate stone architecture. Salento extends towards both the Adriatic and Ionian coasts; allow time between stops.

Gargano

The Gargano peninsula combines coastal towns, cliffs and the wooded interior of Foresta Umbra. It is a separate stage from southern Salento.

Landscape and travel

Bari and Lecce offer different urban experiences, while Alberobello is known for its trulli. Gargano and Salento are far apart, so plan them as separate stages.

Local cuisine and wine

Orecchiette with cime di rapa, focaccia and olive oil are regional staples. Wines include Primitivo di Manduria and those made from Negroamaro.

Wine styles vary by producer; Negroamaro is widely associated with southern Puglia.
