Calabria: what to see and how to travel

Calabria forms the southern tip of mainland Italy between the Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas. The picture shows the historic center of Tropey above the coast.

Tropea

The historic center stands above the Tyrrhenian coast and the viewpoints offer views of the sea. The well-known silhouette is created by the sanctuary of Santa Maria dell'Isola on the rock.

Scilla

The fishing district of Chianalea lies just by the water below Ruffo Castle. The Strait of Messina can be seen from the coast; take coastal distances into account when planning the crossing.

Sila and Aspromonte

The inland mountains show a different face of Calabria than the beaches. Forests, lakes and mountain villages invite you to trips that depend on the weather and the season.

Landscape and road

Calabria forms the southern tip of mainland Italy between the Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas. The Sila and Aspromonte mountain ranges rise a short distance from the coast, so the environment changes rapidly.

Tropea and the coast around Capo Vaticano can be combined into one area; Scilla and the Straits of Messina lie further south. When crossing the mountains, take turns and follow the conditions of the park on hikes.

Local cuisine and wine

Calabria is famous for its red onions from around Tropea and the distinctive spicy sausage 'nduja. Vegetables, pasta and fish also appear in the kitchen; Better check the hotness of a particular dish.

Famous wine regions include Cirò on the Ionian side. Local wines are made in a variety of styles, so it's a good idea to compare labels and producers.

What to taste on the way

’Nduja is a spreadable spicy sausage associated with the Spilinga region. It is used in small quantities on bread and in sauces; her distinct hotness may not suit everyone.
