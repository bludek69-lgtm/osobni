ITALY · REGIONS

Basilicata

In Matera, houses and rock fit so closely together that the city changes shape as you walk.

Beyond it lie rolling inland country and the rocky peaks of the Lucanian Dolomites.

I would explore Basilicata as a journey through several landscapes, rather than a quick detour for one photograph.

Examples of places: Matera · Potenza.

Why choose this region?

Basilicata reaches both the Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas, but much of its character lies inland.

I recommend combining Matera with one mountain area and allowing extra time on minor roads.

Matera is the starting point for exploring the eastern part of the region; the capital of Basilicata is Potenza.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: I would choose spring or autumn for Matera. In summer, break the visit into shorter walks: exposed viewpoints and stone steps feel very different at midday.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Check Ferrovie Appulo Lucane for connections to Matera. For several inland towns, compare driving with actual bus services before committing to an itinerary.
  • What to check: Check luggage access before booking. Historic accommodation may involve steps and a walk from parking; a short straight-line distance can be misleading.

What to taste

Typical of the region are dried peperoni cruschi peppers, often served with local dishes, and pastries or cheeses from the mountain regions.

The composition and appearance of meals varies by municipality.

Aglianico del Vulture is a wine associated with the surroundings of the extinct volcano Vulture in the north of the region.

When choosing, focus on specific origin and vintage.
