Lombardy: what to see and how to travel

Lombardy connects Milan with the Alpine valleys and Lakes Como and Garda. The Duomo of Milan in the photo stands on Piazza del Duomo and is one of the most distinctive buildings of the city.

On this page: Milan · Bergamo Alta · Lakes Como and Garda

Milan

Piazza del Duomo connects the cathedral with the Galleria Vittoria Emanuele II. Other stops may include Sforza Castle or neighborhoods outside the busiest center.

Bergamo Alta

The Upper Town lies above the newer part of Bergamo and is surrounded by walls. The alleys lead to the Piazza Vecchia; check access and parking options in advance upon arrival.

Lakes Como and Garda

The north of the region includes the lakes below the Alps and the west bank of the Garda. When traveling between coastal cities, consider shipping and allow for seasonal changes in timetables.

Landscape and road

Lombardy passes from the plain around Milan to the Alpine valleys and Lakes Como, Maggiore and Garda. Bergamo and Brescia offer historic cities between the metropolis and the mountain landscape.

Milan and Bergamo can be explored by train; for lakes, choose a specific shore and compare boat and rail connections. For mountain trips, follow seasonal access, weather and distance from the station.

Local cuisine and wine

Risotto alla milanese is associated with saffron; in Lombardy you can also find polenta and various cheeses. Stuffed tortelli di zucca pasta appears around Mantua.

Franciacorta is an area known for sparkling wines produced using the traditional method. There are other, different wine regions around the lakes and in Valtellina.

What to taste on the way

Milanese risotto can be recognized by the saffron. But Lombardy also has mountain cuisine: buckwheat pizzoccheri are served in Valtellina, while there is no shortage of fish dishes around the lakes.
