Umbria: what to see and how to travel

Inland Umbria is a region of hills, olive groves and historic towns. In the photo, Assisi is associated with St. Francis; the regional center is Perugia.

On this page: Perugia · Assisi · Orvieto and Lake Trasimeno

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Perugia →

Perugia

The historic center is located on a hill and its main area is Piazza IV Novembre. Narrow streets and viewpoints are suitable for walking.

Assisi

The city associated with Saint Francis is famous for its basilica and stone streets. The basilica and other monuments have their own visiting rules, which it is good to check in advance.

Orvieto and Lake Trasimeno

Orvieto stands on a rocky hill and its cathedral is one of the city's distinctive buildings. Lake Trasimeno in the western part of the region offers a different type of stopover between towns and nature.

Landscape and road

Umbria lies inland between the hills and valleys of central Italy. The landscape is made up of historic hill towns, olive groves and the area around Lake Trasimeno.

Perugia, Assisi and smaller towns are wise to divide into several days. When traveling by car, follow the restrictions in the historic centers and check the current boat connections to visit the lake.

Local cuisine and wine

The region is associated with olive oil, pulses and truffles; sausages also traditionally appear around Norcia. The availability of truffles and some dishes depends on the season.

Orvieto is a famous white wine region; red wine from the Sagrantino variety is produced in the vicinity of Montefalco.

What to taste on the way

In the surroundings of Montefalco, you can get to know wines from the Sagrantino variety. Legumes, olive oil and truffles complement Umbrian cuisine; for tastings and winery visits, check the conditions and mode of transport in advance.
