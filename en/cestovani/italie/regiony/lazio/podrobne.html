Lazio: what to know and how to travel

Lazio surrounds Rome and extends to the Tyrrhenian Sea. In the capital, ancient monuments meet baroque squares and fountains; the picture shows the Trevi Fountain.

On this page: Rome · Tivoli · Lake Bracciano

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Tivoli →

Rome

The ancient monuments around the Colosseum and the Roman Forum can be combined with a walk to the squares and fountains of the historic center. The areas are vast, so it is practical to divide your visit into several parts.

Tivoli

In the city east of Rome lies the Villa d'Este with its gardens and fountains. In the wider area, there is also the archaeological site of Hadrian's Villa; check the entrances and transport between them in advance.

Lake Bracciano

The lake of volcanic origin and the surrounding towns offer a quieter stop outside the capital. It is advisable to check the conditions of access to water and local transport according to the date.

Landscape and road

Lazio stretches from the Tyrrhenian coast through Rome to the hilly interior of the Apennines. Lakes Bracciano and Bolsena, for example, are of volcanic origin; historic gardens and villas complement the city's monuments.

Divide Rome into several walking areas and take into account local transport. Tivoli and Lake Bracciano lend themselves to separate trips; check connections, entrances and opening hours according to the date.

Local cuisine and wine

Roman cuisine is associated with pasta cacio e pepe, amatriciana and carbonara. It often uses pecorino romano; the offer in markets and restaurants changes according to the season.

Frascati is a famous wine region around Rome. With food, you can compare local white wines by variety and producer.

What to taste on the way

Roman cuisine is famous for pasta carbonara, amatriciana and cacio e pepe. Each of the dishes has a different base: for example, cacio e pepe is based on pecorino romano cheese and pepper.
