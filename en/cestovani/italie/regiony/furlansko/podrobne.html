Friuli-Venezia Giulia: what to see and how to travel

Friuli-Venezia Giulia is located on the borders with Austria and Slovenia. Trieste in the picture is a port on the Adriatic Sea; the north of the region passes into the alpine landscape.

On this page: Trieste · Miramar · Aquileia and Grado

Trieste

The port city has a large square, Piazza Unità d'Italia, open to the sea. The walk can be combined with the waterfront, cafes and the view from the hill of San Giusto.

Miramar

Miramare Castle stands on the coast northwest of Trieste and is surrounded by a park. On the way, leave time for a walk in the garden and verify access to the interiors.

Aquileia and Grado

Aquileia preserves Roman and early Christian monuments including a basilica with mosaics. Nearby Grado adds a lagoon, an old center and the Adriatic coast.

Landscape and road

The Alps, the karst landscape above Trieste and the Adriatic coast meet on the northeastern edge of Italy. The region borders Austria and Slovenia, which is also evident in local traditions.

Trieste and Miramare Castle can be combined during a shore day; Aquileia and Grado form another stop towards the west. Plan the mountain areas separately and monitor the conditions of the passes before the trip.

Local cuisine and wine

Frico is usually prepared from cheese and potatoes, iota is a soup combined with beans and cabbage. San Daniele is famous for its local ham; Central European influences are also evident in the Trieste area.

In the wine regions of Collio and other parts of the region you will encounter white wines including the Friulano and Ribolla Gialla varieties.

What to taste on the way

Frico is made from cheese and exists in both soft and crispy forms; some versions add potatoes and onions. Alpine, Slovenian and Italian influences meet in the border cuisine.
