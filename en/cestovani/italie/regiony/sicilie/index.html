Sicily

At Palermo’s markets, stallholders’ voices compete with displays of fish, vegetables and freshly fried food.

On the eastern side of Sicily, Etna takes over the horizon.

I would explore the island in sections: the journey between these two experiences deserves more than a hurried transfer.

Why choose this region?

Ancient cities, Arab and Norman traces, baroque towns and volcanic landscapes each need time.

For a first visit I would choose east or west and stay several nights in one area. A whole-island circuit needs enough days for real stops.

The island offers cities, archaeological monuments and a volcanic landscape. For a longer trip, it's a good idea to allow enough time for transfers.

Palermo, Cefalù and other cities can be complemented by a trip to archaeological monuments.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: For towns and archaeological sites, I would choose April, May or autumn. In summer, explore exposed places early and use the hottest hours for shade or interiors.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Compare trains and buses between main towns, checking actual journey times. A car can help with smaller places; check restricted access zones and parking before entering historic centres.
  • What to check: Plan Etna around current access restrictions and the managing authority’s instructions. Summit access changes: an old itinerary or summit photograph is not proof that the route is open today.

What to taste

Arancini or arancine are stuffed and fried rice specialties; the name varies on the island.

Other well-known flavors include cannoli and dishes with aubergine, fish or pistachios.

On the slopes of Etna, wines are produced at different altitudes, in the west of the island Marsala is famous. Check the label for origin and style.
