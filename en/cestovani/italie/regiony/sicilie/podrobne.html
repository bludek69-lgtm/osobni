Sicily: what to know and how to travel

Sicily is the largest island in the Mediterranean and has its own varied history and cuisine. Pictured is coastal Cefalù; other prominent places are Palermo and Etna volcano.

On this page: Palermo · Cefalu · Etna and archaeological sites

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Palermo →

Palermo

The Sicilian metropolis combines bustling markets, historic streets and monuments from different periods. You can choose the cathedral and the Norman palace to visit according to time and interest.

Cefalu

The coastal town is famous for its cathedral and the rock massif of La Rocca above the rooftops. The streets of the old town lead to the sea and the smaller port.

Etna and archaeological sites

Etna is an active volcano; access may vary depending on activity and weather. The island also offers Greek and Roman monuments, for example in the area of ​​Agrigento and Syracuse.

Landscape and road

Sicily combines the coast, historic cities, inland hills and the volcanic landscape of Mount Etna. It is vast and between the west and east of the island it is worthwhile to divide the journey into several parts.

Plan Palermo and the west of the island separately from Catania, Syracuse and Etna. At the volcano, monitor current alerts, availability and guide requirements; don't plan long moves based on distance alone.

Local cuisine and wine

Arancini or arancine are stuffed and fried rice specialties; the name varies on the island. Other well-known flavors include cannoli and dishes with aubergine, fish or pistachios.

On the slopes of Etna, wines are produced at different altitudes, in the west of the island Marsala is famous. Check the label for origin and style.

What to taste on the way

Arancini are breaded and fried rice specialties with various fillings. On the island, their shape and name change; in addition to savory cuisine, cannoli and granita are also worth tasting.
