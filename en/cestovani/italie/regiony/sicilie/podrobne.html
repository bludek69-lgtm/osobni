Sicily: what to know and how to travel

At Palermo’s markets, stallholders’ voices compete with displays of fish, vegetables and freshly fried food.

On the eastern side of Sicily, Etna takes over the horizon.

I would explore the island in sections: the journey between these two experiences deserves more than a hurried transfer.

Why choose this region?

Ancient cities, Arab and Norman traces, baroque towns and volcanic landscapes each need time.

For a first visit I would choose east or west and stay several nights in one area. A whole-island circuit needs enough days for real stops.

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Palermo →

Palermo

The Sicilian metropolis combines bustling markets, historic streets and monuments from different periods.

You can choose the cathedral and the Norman palace to visit according to time and interest.

Cefalu

The coastal town is famous for its cathedral and the rock massif of La Rocca above the rooftops. The streets of the old town lead to the sea and the smaller port.

Etna and archaeological sites

Etna is an active volcano; access may vary depending on activity and weather.

The island also offers Greek and Roman monuments, for example in the area of ​​Agrigento and Syracuse.

Two detours beyond the main stops

Erice

The stone town above Trapani can have different weather from the coast below.

Account for the ascent and current transport operation, not just the short distance on the map.

Scicli

I would include this southeastern baroque town in the same part of the trip as Ragusa and Modica. Give it a walk of its own instead of rushing through all three.

Landscape and road

Sicily combines the coast, historic cities, inland hills and the volcanic landscape of Mount Etna.

It is vast and between the west and east of the island it is worthwhile to divide the journey into several parts.

Plan Palermo and the west of the island separately from Catania, Syracuse and Etna.

At the volcano, monitor current alerts, availability and guide requirements; don't plan long moves based on distance alone.

What to taste

Arancini or arancine are stuffed and fried rice specialties; the name varies on the island.

Other well-known flavors include cannoli and dishes with aubergine, fish or pistachios.

On the slopes of Etna, wines are produced at different altitudes, in the west of the island Marsala is famous. Check the label for origin and style.

How I would choose a meal

Arancini are breaded and fried rice specialties with various fillings.

On the island, their shape and name change; in addition to savory cuisine, cannoli and granita are also worth tasting.

I would choose arancini or arancine for the filling rather than argue about one correct name. For cannoli, ask when they are filled and what is inside.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: For towns and archaeological sites, I would choose April, May or autumn. In summer, explore exposed places early and use the hottest hours for shade or interiors.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Compare trains and buses between main towns, checking actual journey times. A car can help with smaller places; check restricted access zones and parking before entering historic centres.
  • What to check: Plan Etna around current access restrictions and the managing authority’s instructions. Summit access changes: an old itinerary or summit photograph is not proof that the route is open today.
