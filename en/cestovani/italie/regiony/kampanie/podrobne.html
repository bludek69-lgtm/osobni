Campania: what to see and how to travel

Campania connects the Gulf of Naples, Mount Vesuvius and the Amalfi Coast. The photo shows Positano between the mountains and the sea; important archaeological sites in the region include Pompeii.

On this page: Naples · Pompeii and Vesuvius · Amalfi coast

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: Naples →

Naples

The historic center, Piazza del Plebiscito and the coast offer different views of the city. Leave time for ordinary streets and local cuisine and expect busier traffic.

Pompeii and Vesuvius

The archaeological site of Pompeii depicts the city buried by the eruption of Vesuvius. Allow enough time for the tour and check the current conditions and access at the ascent to the volcano.

Amalfi coast

The steep coast connects the towns of Amalfi, Positano and other villages. The roads tend to be narrow and busy; compare bus and boat connections according to the season.

Landscape and road

Campania connects the Gulf of Naples, Mount Vesuvius, islands and the steep Amalfi Coast with a mountainous interior. Pompeii and the volcano are separate places, although they can be connected thematically during one trip.

Take your time in Naples; Pompeii requires a longer walking tour. Plan the Amalfi Coast according to traffic and season: narrow roads and parking can be busy, so compare bus and boat connections.

Local cuisine and wine

Neapolitan pizza is associated with the city of Naples, the region is also home to mozzarella di bufala campana. You can find fish and lemon specialties near the coast, and vegetable and pasta dishes inland.

Local wines include white Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo; other wine regions also follow the volcanic soils. When tasting, follow the origin indicated on the label.

What to taste on the way

In Naples, pizza margherita is worth tasting, but also simple marinara. When choosing a business, it will help to look at the local offer and opening hours; popular pizzerias may have queues.
