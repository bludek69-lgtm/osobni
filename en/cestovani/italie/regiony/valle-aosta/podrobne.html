Valle d'Aosta: what to see and how to travel

The Aosta Valley is surrounded by the highest alpine massifs, including Mont Blanc and Monte Rosa. The landscape consists of mountain valleys, stone villages and medieval castles; the picture shows Fénis Castle.

Aosta

The regional center preserves Roman monuments including the Arch of Augustus and the remains of a theater. The town can serve as a starting point for exploring the surrounding valleys.

Fenis Castle

The medieval castle is known for its tower system and inner courtyard. Before visiting, check opening hours and tour method.

Gran Paradiso and the high Alps

Gran Paradiso National Park protects the mountain environment at the border of Valle d'Aosta and Piedmont. Choose the routes based on your experience, the weather and the current instructions of the park administration.

Landscape and road

The smallest Italian region lies in the Alps near the borders with France and Switzerland. The valleys around Aosta are surrounded by high peaks and the side valleys lead to distinct mountain areas.

Aosta is suitable for a city stop and a starting point for planning the surroundings. Choose a specific valley for Gran Paradiso; check the conditions of hikes, access roads and the operation of the cable cars according to the time of year.

Local cuisine and wine

Fontina cheese is used in mountain cuisine, for example in fondue and concia polenta. Other dishes include bread and cheese soups or carbonade stew.

The vineyards on the mountain slopes give the local wines a different character depending on the location and variety. When traveling by car, plan the tasting with a safe return.

What to taste on the way

Fontina is a cheese closely associated with the Aosta Valley and also appears in warm mountain dishes. During your visit, you can compare local cheeses or taste polenta; the specific composition varies according to the recipe.
