Veneto: what to see and how to travel

In Venice, your next turn depends on a bridge or a boat stop.

Beyond the lagoon, Veneto changes into vineyards, the plain around Padua and the rocky Dolomites.

I would give the cities and the countryside separate parts of the trip, rather than returning to the same car park every evening.

Why choose this region?

The lagoon tells only part of Veneto’s story. Verona, Vicenza and Padua deserve their own time, while vineyards and foothills suit a slower journey.

I would combine two cities with one rural area.

Venice and the lagoon

Historic Venice can be explored on foot between St. Mark's Square, the Rialto and the less busy districts.

Go to the islands of Murano and Burano by water transport according to the current timetable.

Verona

In Piazza Bra stands the Roman Arena; you can continue on foot from there to Piazza delle Erbe and the Adige river.

The historic center is suitable for a slower walk without a car.

Dolomites and Lake Garda

The north of the region includes the mountain valleys around Cortina d'Ampezzo.

To the west lies the eastern bank of the Garda with towns like Malcesine; the conditions of cable cars and boats change according to the season.

Two detours beyond the main stops

Arquà Petrarca

This village in the Euganean Hills combines stone buildings with memories of the poet Petrarch.

I would give it a relaxed visit after a city day, rather than squeeze it into an evening.

Montagnana

The walls make more sense as a complete defensive system than as a backdrop to one gate. Walk around part of them, then stop in the centre.

Landscape and road

Veneto includes the lagoon and the Adriatic coast, the lowlands around the rivers and the mountainous north with the Dolomites.

It touches Lake Garda in the west; a city trip and a mountain vacation have a different rhythm here.

Discover Venice on foot and use water transport between the islands.

Verona can be connected to the eastern shore of Garda, while in the Dolomites around Cortina, set aside some time and check seasonal traffic.

What to taste

The menu may include risi e bisi, a rice and pea dish, or baccalà mantecato.

In Venice, a small portion of cicchetti is worth knowing; their form depends on the specific company.

Prosecco is also associated with the hilly area between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene. Valpolicella near Verona is famous for red wines including Amarone.

How I would choose a meal

In Venice, look for small bars called bàcari that serve cicchetti – small bites with various toppings.

They can serve as a stop during the walk, but the offer and price vary by establishment.

Choose a few different cicchetti and compare what each bàcaro offers. For fish dishes, ask about bones and check whether the price is per portion or by weight.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: Spring or autumn would be my choice for cities. Plan mountain outings separately around altitude and trail conditions; weather in Venice says little about the Dolomites.
  • Cars, trains and connections: I would first plan Venice, Padua, Vicenza and Verona by train. A car is more useful for selected countryside stops than for moving between historic centres.
  • What to check: Check Venice’s official access-contribution rules for your actual visit date. The calendar changes, and the same payment obligation does not apply to every visitor.
