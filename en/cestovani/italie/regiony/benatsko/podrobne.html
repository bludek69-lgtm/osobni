Veneto

Veneto stretches from the Dolomites past Verona to the Adriatic. Venice, pictured here, grew across islands in the lagoon, where canals and pedestrian lanes shape everyday life.

Venice and the lagoon

Explore Venice on foot and by vaporetto, from St Mark’s area to quieter neighbourhoods. Lagoon islands require extra travel time; check current boat services.

Verona

Walk from Piazza Bra and the Arena into the historic streets around Piazza delle Erbe. Bridges across the Adige offer views back towards the old town.

Dolomites and Lake Garda

The Venetian Dolomites and the eastern shore of Lake Garda are distinct areas. Check weather, access and transport before planning a mountain or lakeside outing.

Landscape and travel

Venice’s canals, Verona’s historic streets and the Dolomites are distinct destinations. Consider trains between cities and check mountain weather before setting out.

Local cuisine and wine

In Venice, bàcari serve small bites called cicchetti; elsewhere try polenta or risotto. Valpolicella and Prosecco are among the better-known regional wines.

Valpolicella wines come from the hills near Verona; Prosecco has its own designated production areas.
