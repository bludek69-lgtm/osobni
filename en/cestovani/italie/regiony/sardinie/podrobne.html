Sardinia: what to know and how to travel

Sardinia is a Mediterranean island with a long coastline and a mountainous interior. The collage depicts places in Cagliari, the capital of the region in the south of the island.

Cagliari

The southern metropolis offers the historic district of Castello above the city and views towards the bay. The walk can be complemented by a visit to the Poetto coast.

Alghero

The town in the northwest of the island has an old center and walls by the sea. Nearby lies the promontory of Capo Caccia; boat and other approaches to the Grotta di Nettuno depend on conditions.

Hinterlands and nuraghas

In Sardinia, there are stone nuragha tower structures, reminiscent of the ancient settlement of the island. When traveling inland, expect longer distances and check access to the selected area.

Landscape and road

Sardinia is a vast island with a rugged coastline, a mountainous interior and archaeological monuments of the Nuragic culture. The northern archipelago of La Maddalena as well as individual protected beaches have special rules.

Don't choose accommodation based only on a photo of one beach: moving around the island can take longer. For selected beaches and protected areas, check the entry, possible reservation and rules in advance; don't take anything from nature.

Local cuisine and wine

Among the local dishes, you can try stuffed culurgiones pasta, pane carasau thin bread or sheep cheeses. The menu varies between the coast and the interior.

Cannonau is the island's famous red variety and Vermentino is used for white wines. The specific character depends on the region and the manufacturer.

What to taste on the way

Culurgiones are filled pastas associated especially with the Ogliastra region. Both the filling and the method of administration differ; you can meet with potatoes, cheese and mint or with tomato sauce.
