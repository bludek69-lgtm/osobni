Abruzzo

Abruzzo lies between the Adriatic and the high Apennines. Rocca Calascio castle, pictured here, stands in mountain scenery; Gran Sasso is among the region’s prominent massifs.

Places worth exploring

Detailed guide: L’Aquila (in Czech) →

L’Aquila

L’Aquila is a mountain city and a base for the interior. Walk around Piazza del Duomo and the Fountain of 99 Spouts; reconstruction after the 2009 earthquake continues. Check which sites are open.

Rocca Calascio

The stone fortress stands above a high Apennine landscape. The approach is exposed to weather and the walk involves a climb; choose footwear and timing accordingly.

Gran Sasso

Campo Imperatore is a broad plateau beneath the Gran Sasso massif. Plan hikes around the season, weather and current park guidance.

Landscape and travel

The Apennines rise inland from the Adriatic. L’Aquila, Gran Sasso and the mountain villages make a good starting point for exploring; allow time for winding roads and check trail conditions.

Local cuisine and wine

Try arrosticini, small sheep-meat skewers, and pasta alla chitarra. Montepulciano d’Abruzzo is a regional wine, distinct from the Tuscan town of Montepulciano.

Trebbiano d’Abruzzo is among the local white wines.
