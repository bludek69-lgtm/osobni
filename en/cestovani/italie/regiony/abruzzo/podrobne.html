Abruzzo: what to know and how to travel

Abruzzo stretches between the Adriatic and the high Apennines. The Rocca Calascio castle in the picture stands in a mountain landscape; among the prominent massifs of the region is the Gran Sasso.

On this page: L'Aquila · Rocca Calascio · Gran Sasso

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: L’Aquila →

L'Aquila

The metropolis of the region lies in the mountains and its historic center is gradually being restored after the earthquake. The walk includes the Piazza del Duomo and the surrounding streets; it is advisable to check the condition and accessibility of individual monuments in advance.

Rocca Calascio

The ruins of the mountain fortress stand high above the surrounding landscape. The trip is interesting because of the views of the Apennines; depending on the weather and parking, expect to walk uphill.

Gran Sasso

The mountain massif offers high peaks and open plains around Campo Imperatore. Adapt the route selection to the time of year, experience and current conditions.

Landscape and road

Abruzzo rises from the Adriatic coast to the Apennines. The Gran Sasso mountain massif, the open plateau of Campo Imperatore and the wooded areas to the south show a significantly different face of the region than the seaside towns.

Plan the coast and mountains separately: moving inland takes time and high mountain roads can be limited depending on the season. Rocca Calascio link with surrounding villages; check the weather, access and difficulty with the relevant park administration before the hike.

Local cuisine and wine

Local dishes include arrosticini, small skewers of mutton, and maccheroni alla chitarra pasta made with a stringed instrument. You can also find cheeses, pulses and coastal fish dishes in different parts of the region.

The famous wine is Montepulciano d'Abruzzo from the Montepulciano variety; Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo is pink. Do not confuse the variety and name of the wine with the Tuscan town of Montepulciano.

What to taste on the way

Arrosticini are small skewers traditionally prepared from mutton. On the way inland it pays to look for local trattorias; in addition to meat, you can also come across cheeses and legumes from mountain regions.
