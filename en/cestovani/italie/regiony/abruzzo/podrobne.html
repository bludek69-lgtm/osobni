Abruzzo: what to know and how to travel

The road opens onto the mountain plateau of Campo Imperatore, while wooden fishing platforms called trabocchi stand over the sea along the coast.

These are two very different sides of Abruzzo. I recommend giving each its own part of the trip rather than rushing between them.

Why choose this region?

I would come for the space between mountain villages, pastures and national parks.

Coastal life centres on beaches and harbours; inland journeys reward a slower pace. A seaside base will not suit every mountain outing.

Places worth knowing

Detailed guide: L’Aquila →

L'Aquila

The metropolis of the region lies in the mountains and its historic center is gradually being restored after the earthquake.

The walk includes the Piazza del Duomo and the surrounding streets; it is advisable to check the condition and accessibility of individual monuments in advance.

Rocca Calascio

The ruins of the mountain fortress stand high above the surrounding landscape.

The trip is interesting because of the views of the Apennines; depending on the weather and parking, expect to walk uphill.

Gran Sasso

The mountain massif offers high peaks and open plains around Campo Imperatore. Adapt the route selection to the time of year, experience and current conditions.

Two detours beyond the main stops

Santo Stefano di Sessanio

Stone houses below Gran Sasso and locally grown lentils tell a story of mountain farming. I would allow time for a village walk and lunch.

Scanno

The old town and nearby lake offer a change after a mountain hike. Another summit is not essential to a rewarding day.

Landscape and road

Abruzzo rises from the Adriatic coast to the Apennines.

The Gran Sasso mountain massif, the open plateau of Campo Imperatore and the wooded areas to the south show a significantly different face of the region than the seaside towns.

Plan the coast and mountains separately: moving inland takes time and high mountain roads can be limited depending on the season.

Rocca Calascio link with surrounding villages; check the weather, access and difficulty with the relevant park administration before the hike.

What to taste

Local dishes include arrosticini, small skewers of mutton, and maccheroni alla chitarra pasta made with a stringed instrument.

You can also find cheeses, pulses and coastal fish dishes in different parts of the region.

The famous wine is Montepulciano d'Abruzzo from the Montepulciano variety; Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo is pink.

Do not confuse the variety and name of the wine with the Tuscan town of Montepulciano.

How I would choose a meal

Arrosticini are small skewers traditionally prepared from mutton.

On the way inland it pays to look for local trattorias; in addition to meat, you can also come across cheeses and legumes from mountain regions.

I would order a small first course alongside the skewers rather than several meat dishes. For a meat-free meal, ask whether lentils or soup contain meat stock.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: For towns and lower ground, I would choose May, June or September. Plan high mountain walks around actual snow conditions and the forecast, rather than the season on the coast.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Start by checking trains for coastal stops. For several mountain villages in one day, I would consider a car. Check the final bus connection before booking accommodation.
  • What to check: Check the chosen trail and park restrictions before setting out. Stay on marked routes and keep your distance from wildlife.
