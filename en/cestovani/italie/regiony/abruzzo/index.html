Abruzzo

The road opens onto the mountain plateau of Campo Imperatore, while wooden fishing platforms called trabocchi stand over the sea along the coast.

These are two very different sides of Abruzzo. I recommend giving each its own part of the trip rather than rushing between them.

Examples of places: L'Aquila · Gran Sasso.

Why choose this region?

I would come for the space between mountain villages, pastures and national parks.

Coastal life centres on beaches and harbours; inland journeys reward a slower pace. A seaside base will not suit every mountain outing.

In addition to mountain villages, the region also offers the Adriatic coast. In the mountains, it is advisable to adapt the route to the weather and the season.

Practical travel tips

  • When to go: For towns and lower ground, I would choose May, June or September. Plan high mountain walks around actual snow conditions and the forecast, rather than the season on the coast.
  • Cars, trains and connections: Start by checking trains for coastal stops. For several mountain villages in one day, I would consider a car. Check the final bus connection before booking accommodation.
  • What to check: Check the chosen trail and park restrictions before setting out. Stay on marked routes and keep your distance from wildlife.

What to taste

Local dishes include arrosticini, small skewers of mutton, and maccheroni alla chitarra pasta made with a stringed instrument.

You can also find cheeses, pulses and coastal fish dishes in different parts of the region.

The famous wine is Montepulciano d'Abruzzo from the Montepulciano variety; Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo is pink.

Do not confuse the variety and name of the wine with the Tuscan town of Montepulciano.
