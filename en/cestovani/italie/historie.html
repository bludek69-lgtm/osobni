ITALY · HISTORY AND CULTURE

The history of Italy: stories left in the landscape

From the Greek cities and Rome through the commercial republics to the unification of the country. You can connect individual periods with places that can be visited.

Antiquity · Middle Ages · Renaissance · Unification · Modern Italy

Greek settlements and ancient Rome

On the coast of southern Italy and Sicily, Greek cities arose in antiquity; their temples and theaters remind of the connection of the peninsula with the Mediterranean. At the same time, other peoples lived in the territory of today's Italy, for example the Etruscans in the central part of the peninsula.

From a city on the Tiber, Rome gradually became the center of a vast empire. Roads, aqueducts and public buildings connected cities and distant provinces. You can also find the remains of ancient streets and houses in Pompeii, which were buried by the eruption of Vesuvius in 79.

The Middle Ages: Cities, Trade and the Sea

After the collapse of the Western Roman Empire, the political map was transformed. Byzantines, Lombards, Normans and other powers were active in different parts of the peninsula. Cities in the north and center gradually gained importance through crafts, trade and institutions of their own.

Venice and Genoa were building trade links across the sea. Inland, cities such as Florence and Siena competed. Their squares, town halls, walls and churches still show that medieval Italy was not one state.

Siena → · Venice → · Liguria →

Renaissance and new views of the world

In the cities of central and northern Italy, since the late Middle Ages, the study of antiquity, literature, fine arts, and new ways of depicting space developed. Artists and builders worked for city institutions, the church and wealthy families.

Florence is associated with Medici patronage; Rome later attracted artists of the papal court. The Renaissance was not the only style in the whole country: it was followed by Baroque and regional building traditions.

Tuscany → · Lazio →

From many states to a united Italy

Even in the first half of the 19th century, the peninsula was divided between various kingdoms and other states. The unification movement, known as the risorgimento, combined political negotiations, uprisings, and military expeditions.

In 1861, the Kingdom of Italy was established; the process of unification continued with the annexation of other territories and in 1870 also Rome. Reminders of this period can be found in the names of squares and streets dedicated to Garibaldi, Cavour or Victor Emmanuel II.

Piedmont and Turin →

Wars, the Republic and today

Italy was affected by both world wars and the period of fascist dictatorship. After the Second World War, voters decided on a republic in a referendum; the new republican constitution entered into force in 1948.

Economic changes, migration between regions and the development of travel have transformed life in cities and in the countryside. When visiting, it is useful to perceive not only the monument itself, but also its surroundings and the people who live there today.
