How to read Italian words

Read each spelling rule, then play the calm Italian voice. Each recording says the displayed words in order.

gg and double consonants

Double consonants are held longer. Before e, i, gg has the “j” sound of “judge” in oggi. Length can change the meaning: pala means shovel, palla means ball.

oggi · pala · palla

gn and gli

gn is one sound, like “ny” in “canyon”: gnocchi. Before a vowel, gli is a soft palatal /ʎ/: famiglia. Do not pronounce g and l separately.

gnocchi · famiglia

sc and sch

Before e, i, sc sounds like “sh”: pesce. Before a, o, u, it sounds like “sk”: scala. sch keeps “sk” before e, i: schema.

pesce · scala · schema

c and ch

Before e, i, c sounds like “ch” in “chair”: cena. Adding h makes it a hard “k”: chi. Plain c is also hard before a, o, u.

cena · chi

g and gh

Before e, i, g sounds like “j” in “judge”: gelato. gh keeps the hard “g”: spaghetti. Plain g is also hard before a, o, u.

gelato · spaghetti

Stress at the end

An accent mark on the final vowel shows that the last syllable is stressed. Listen to città and caffè; do not stress their first syllable.

città · caffè
