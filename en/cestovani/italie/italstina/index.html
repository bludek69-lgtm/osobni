LISTENING AND PRONUNCIATION

How to read Italian words

Look at the group of letters, read the example and play the calm Italian voice. The audio in each card says the words in the order listed.

gg and double consonants

Double consonants are held longer. Before e, i, gg sounds like the j in ‘jam’, as in oggi. Length can change meaning: pala means shovel, while palla means ball.

oggi · pala · palla

gn and gli

gn forms a single sound similar to Czech ‘ň’: gnocchi. Before a vowel, gli represents the palatal sound /ʎ/, which has no exact Czech equivalent: famiglia. Do not pronounce g and l separately.

gnocchi · famiglia

sc and sch

Before e, i, sc sounds like English ‘sh’: pesce. Before a, o, u, it sounds like ‘sk’: scala. Before e or i, sch keeps the ‘sk’ sound: schema.

pesce · scala · schema

c and ch

Before e, i, c sounds like English ‘ch’: cena. Adding h changes it to ‘k’: chi. Before a, o, u, c also has a hard ‘k’ sound.

cena · chi

g and gh

Before e, i, g sounds like the j in ‘jam’: gelato. Before e or i, gh keeps a hard ‘g’: spaghetti. Before a, o, u, g also has a hard sound.

gelato · spaghetti

Stress on the final syllable

An accent on the final vowel marks stress on the last syllable. Listen to città and caffè; do not stress their first syllable.

città · caffè
