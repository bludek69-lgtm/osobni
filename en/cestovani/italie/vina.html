Italian wines by region

A concise guide to regional grapes and wine areas. Styles depend on the producer and vintage; if you travel by car, choose a driver who does not drink.

Abruzzo

Trebbiano d’Abruzzo is among the local white wines.

Food and wine in Abruzzo →

Apulia

Wine styles vary by producer; Negroamaro is widely associated with southern Puglia.

Food and wine in Apulia →

Basilicata

Aglianico is the grape; Vulture identifies the area linked to the wine.

Food and wine in Basilicata →

Veneto

Valpolicella wines come from the hills near Verona; Prosecco has its own designated production areas.

Food and wine in Veneto →

Emilia-Romagna

Lambrusco refers to a family of grapes and wines, with different styles and degrees of sweetness.

Food and wine in Emilia-Romagna →

Friuli Venezia Giulia

Friulano, Ribolla Gialla and Pinot Grigio are among the grapes encountered in regional wines.

Food and wine in Friuli Venezia Giulia →

Calabria

Cirò is a Calabrian wine area; grape and style depend on the individual wine.

Food and wine in Calabria →

Campania

Among regional white wines are Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

Food and wine in Campania →

Lazio

Frascati is a wine area in the hills southeast of Rome.

Food and wine in Lazio →

Liguria

The steep coastal vineyards around Cinque Terre produce distinctive local wines.

Food and wine in Liguria →

Lombardy

Franciacorta is known for sparkling wine, while Valtellina is associated with mountain vineyards.

Food and wine in Lombardy →

Marche

Verdicchio is a grape associated with the region’s white wines.

Food and wine in Marche →

Molise

Tintilia is a grape associated with Molise; styles differ among producers.

Food and wine in Molise →

Piedmont

Barolo and Barbaresco are wines made from Nebbiolo in distinct areas of the Langhe.

Food and wine in Piedmont →

Sardinia

Vermentino and Cannonau are among the island’s better-known wine names.

Food and wine in Sardinia →

Sicily

Nero d’Avola and Etna wines represent different parts of the island.

Food and wine in Sicily →

Tuscany

Chianti and Chianti Classico are distinct wine designations; Brunello di Montalcino is made around Montalcino.

Food and wine in Tuscany →

Trentino-Alto Adige

Regional wines include Teroldego and wines from the Gewürztraminer grape.

Food and wine in Trentino-Alto Adige →

Umbria

Montefalco Sagrantino is connected with the Sagrantino grape and the Montefalco area.

Food and wine in Umbria →

Valle d’Aosta

Mountain vineyards produce regional wines at a range of altitudes; styles differ by producer.
