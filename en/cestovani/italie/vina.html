Italian wines by region

Basic orientation in local wines, varieties and regions. The same name can refer to a variety, a wine or a territory; the complete information on the label is decisive.

Jak číst označení

The Protected Designation of Origin links the name of the wine to a specific territory and production rules. The Italian abbreviations DOC and DOCG are related to the protected designation of origin; IGT stands for Geographical Indication. Specific conditions vary by wine name.

Abruzzo

Montepulciano d’Abruzzo refers to wine from Abruzzo; the name of the town of Montepulciano in Tuscany means something else. You can find Trebbiano d'Abruzzo among the local white wines.

Apulia

In Apulia, you will meet Primitivo di Manduria wine and the Negroamaro variety, which is used in the southern part of the region. Both style and taste vary by manufacturer.

Guide to the Apulia region →

Basilicata

Aglianico del Vulture is a red wine associated with the Vulture area in the north of Basilicata; Aglianico is the name of the variety.

Guide to the Basilicata region →

Veneto

Valpolicella and Amarone wines are associated with Veneto; Prosecco is also produced in the area between Veneto and Friuli.

Guide to the Veneto region →

Emilia-Romagna

Lambrusco is a group of local wines and varieties, often sparkling; in Romagna you will meet wines from the Sangiovese variety.

Guide to the Emilia-Romagna region →

Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Friuli is known for its white wines; Friulano is the local name of the variety and part of the designation of some local wines.

Guide to the Friuli-Venezia Giulia region →

Calabria

Cirò is a wine region on the Ionian side of Calabria; the Gaglioppo variety is important for local red wines.

Guide to the Calabria region →

Campania

Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo are names of wines associated with the interior of Campania; both are examples of local white wines.

Guide to the Campania region →

Lazio

Frascati is a wine region in Lazio known mainly for its white wine. It is worth checking the full name and designation of origin on the label.

Guide to the Lazio region →

Liguria

Cinque Terre and Colli di Luni are examples of wine designations associated with the Ligurian coast and its surroundings.

Guide to the Liguria region →

Lombardy

Franciacorta is an area known for sparkling wine; in Valtellina you will meet red wines from the Nebbiolo variety.

Guide to the Lombardy region →

Marche

Verdicchio is a variety associated with the white wines of the Marche; an example is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi.

Guide to the Marche region →

Molise

Biferno and Molise are examples of the region's wine appellations. The specific varieties and style depend on the wine and the producer.

Guide to the Molise region →

Piedmont

Barolo and Barbaresco come from Piedmont and are associated with the Nebbiolo variety. Barbera is another important local variety.

Guide to the Piedmont region →

Sardinia

Cannonau is the name of both the variety and the Sardinian wine; Vermentino di Gallura is an example of a white wine from the northeast of the island.

Sardinia region guide →

Sicily

Marsala is a Sicilian wine associated with the west of the island; Etna wines come from the area around the volcano of the same name.

Guide to the Sicily region →

Tuscany

Chianti and Brunello di Montalcino are wine names from Tuscany; the significant variety of the area is Sangiovese.

Guide to the Tuscany region →

Trentino-Alto Adige

The region produces both white and red wines; local varieties include, for example, Gewürztraminer and Lagrein.

Guide to the Trentino-Alto Adige region →

Umbria

Orvieto is a famous white wine from the area of the city of the same name. Montefalco is also associated with red wines from the Sagrantino variety.

Guide to the region of Umbria →

Valle d'Aosta

In Valle d'Aosta, many varieties are grown on the mountain slopes. The Valle d'Aosta appellation includes different types of local wines.
