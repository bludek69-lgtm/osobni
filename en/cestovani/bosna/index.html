Mostar & Stari Most

A short stop on the way to the sea. Mostar — a city where the stones of the bridge are reflected in the turquoise Neretva and where minarets, church towers and mountains form one of the most unusual panoramas of the Balkans.

What's there

Stari Most — a 16th-century stone arch by the Ottoman builder Mimar Hayruddin. It connects both parts of the city via the Neretva and has become a symbol of the entire country. The original did not survive the war in 1993, the bridge was destroyed by bombing. Today's appearance is a faithful reconstruction from 2004 — stones fished from the river were used again, new parts in identical technique. Since 2005, the bridge has been on the list UNESCO.

The bridge is sometimes still jumped from today — local fighters in swimsuits dive from 24 meters into the cold Neretva for a tourist contribution. Under the bridge is an old market with handicrafts, copper, carpets and coffee in copper jugs.

"Stari Most is not just a bridge — it's a message that things can be built again."
