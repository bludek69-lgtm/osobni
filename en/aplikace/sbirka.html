Why was it created and what can it do?

Why it was created: a friend from work collects coins and stamps as a hobby and had a lot of questions for me — what is this piece, where to find it, how much it sells for. So I built an app for him to make it clear: the piece will be photographed, the app will help him to identify a roughly estimated and will directly link where to check the price. Runs locally, looks like an auction house catalog, no account, no data gone.

Two modes: public demo (sample data only, runs in browser) a full version on my PC with my own database, photos and AI. A real collection never leaves the computer.

AI recognition from photo: I take a picture of a coin or a stamp, the app sends the photo to the AI (Gemini) and it reads the text on the piece and pre-fills the form — category, country, year, denomination, state. Then I just check and save. It can handle both sides at once (front + back). If the photo is blurry or it's not a coin, the app won't fill in anything and will ask for a new photo (like verifying a document).

Where it is sold: I browse catalogs and stores (Numista, Pofis, Colnect, Delcampe...). directly in the application — for the find, I see the name, price and picture, and prices in foreign currency are converted to crowns according to the CNB exchange rate. This is what I am doing with the item price history over time to see where the value goes. It is immediately clear how much the same piece is realistically sold for — not how much someone wants it for.

Help for beginners: a separate page will clearly explain AI (Gemini) — how to set up a free key and how much it costs — as well as phone photography, backups and security. The key for AI can be inserted directly in the application and remains only on the computer.

What affects the price: educational section — what increases the value (higher grade, misprint, certificate, NH glue on stamps) and what decreases it (cleaning, ear, defects). So that one does not buy and sell blindly.

Bulk import: those who already have a list in Excel will upload it at once (CSV / XLSX) instead of clicking one by one.

Detailed list of features
  • Auction house style catalog — dark appearance with gold accents, detail of the item as an auction "lot" (large photo, parameters, valuation).
  • AI recognition from a photo — Gemini reads the text on the coin/stamp and pre-fills the field; obverse + reverse in one step; photo quality control (bad = no rating); answers in Czech.
  • Catalogs directly in the application — viewing Czech and foreign catalogs and stores (coins, stamps) in the application window: name, price, image, link to source. Prices in foreign currency converted to crowns (CNB exchange rate), price history of the item over time. The "realized price takes precedence over the offer" rule.
  • Help for beginners — a page that explains the AI key (Gemini), photography, backups and security; for each internet/AI connection there is a ℹ button with an explanation.
  • What affects the price — factors ↑/↓ separately for coins and separately for stamps + how the price is composed (catalog vs. auction vs. estimate).
  • Simple authentication (since 1.2.6, expanded since then) — quick stamp verification: obverse + reverse, scan quality check directly on the computer, Gemini model selection with price list and price estimate in advance, 14 checks (identification, condition and authenticity, market valuation, business analysis buy/sell, internet search via Google search in Gemini). The result is a report with an indicative price, the level of suspicion of authenticity, a table of checks used, sources from the Internet and consumed tokens, as well as the price of verification (USD and CZK), export to PDF/DOCX. It runs for free as a simulation with no paid calls allowed. The report states only what was supported by a documented source.
  • Series and sheets at once (since 1.3.0) — the entire sheet or sheet is divided into individual stamps (maximum 40 pieces), the faces and reverses are paired and I check and correct them side by side before verification. Each stamp has its own result, the series has its own independent price, and the costs are added up for the entire batch.
  • Documented prices with links (since 1.2.7, extended in 1.9–1.12) — the application searches for offers and realized sales, verifies the series, year and catalog number before searching for prices and shows conditional spreads by condition, variant and unit with links to sources. What cannot be substantiated is not invented — if there is no basis, the application will tell you.
  • Gear gauge (from 1.10.2) — measure serrations directly from the scan and show which holes it counted from; with a ruler scanned to ±0.05. It is not an expert confirmation of the variant.
  • Saving to a collection (since 1.5.0) — the actual analyzed stamps are self-archiving with photos and links to resources; AI suggestions remain unconfirmed, no duplicates occur, and the entire collection can be reversibly moved to the trash.
  • Private Sale (since 1.11.0) — offer guide for Delcampe and eBay.de: German offers, photos, postage, editable conditions, sales and shipping records and offline video tutorials. Offers are posted manually.
  • System Health and Diagnostics (since 1.8.0) — application status and local diagnostic package without photos, prompts, keys and collection data; for each verification, see the model used and the service's response. The spending ceiling for AI can be set directly in Keys & API.
  • Expertise and reviews — a report on the item as an immutable record with an audit trail (nothing can be overwritten, only a new one can be added). AI opinion via Gemini with model selection, comparison with signs of forgery/authenticity from the minutiae database. It's still about preliminary visual assessment, not an official expertise or certificate; authenticity only by verbal level of suspicion, never by percentage — authenticity calibration has not yet taken place.
  • Valuation with evidence — I add a source to the item (catalogue / completed auction / review), the active offer is not included in the average.
  • Photos of items — front/back, card and detail view.
  • Import / export — bulk import from CSV/Excel, export CSV / Excel / PDF / ZIP.
  • Locally and securely — SQLite + photos only on my PC, only code and sample data goes on GitHub. The Windows application starts the backend itself with one click.
