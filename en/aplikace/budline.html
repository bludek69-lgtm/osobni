BudLine Panel

🚍BudLine Panel

An app for bus drivers that keeps track of shifts, rosters and pay. Instead of paper notes and mental arithmetic: I log my shifts and the app calculates what should arrive on the payslip — and at the end of the month I check whether it matches. Everything runs locally on the computer, no cloud, no accounts.

Python SQLite Windows macOS v1.2.103 BETA

What it does

Why it exists: a bus driver's shifts are scattered across rosters, with weekend and holiday bonuses, and at the end of the month there is a payslip that is hard to verify. BudLine is a simple panel for that: I log my shifts (or read them from the driver card), the app adds them up according to the rules and shows what the pay should be. Two of us use it — me and a colleague from work.

Shift and roster records: each shift has a date, times and a type; the app validates the entry (HH:MM time format, no negative numbers) and builds a monthly overview.

Payroll documents: from the logged shifts the app calculates the pay basis according to configured rules (hours, bonuses). At the end of the month you just compare it with the payslip — if something is off, you see exactly where.

Driver card reading: since version 1.2.13 the app can read data directly from the driver's chip card via a reader — less manual typing.

Everything local: data stays on the computer (SQLite). Updates install silently in the background; an upgrade never overwrites user data or settings.

Key features

  • Monthly shift overview — logged shifts roll up into an overview with attendance totals.
  • Payroll calculation — by rules (hours, bonuses); end-of-month comparison with the payslip.
  • Month close & fill from payslip — the home page shows an unclosed month; on close, one button fills items that aren't on the standard roster (fuel and regular bonuses, laundry, CNG) straight from the uploaded payslip.
  • In-app roster editor — create a roster in a table or edit an existing one, batch import (HTML/XML/ZIP), stop autocomplete and a vehicle field (V-code, e.g. "3-axle bus").
  • Compliance assessment — for a single duty roster and for the whole month: does the planned shift meet AETR and Czech Gov. Reg. 589/2006? Day-by-day list of breaches, full and short version, print to PDF.
  • Plan vs reality from the driver card — next to the planned roster you see what the card actually recorded (driving, work, rest) and where it differs; rest periods are evaluated continuously across midnight and card times are converted from UTC to local time.
  • Overall monthly assessment — payslip, driven rosters and the driver card in one view: how much was really driven, how it matches what was paid, and a summary of all breaches.
  • Driving-time checks (AETR) — shifts that may breach road-transport rules are flagged for review; pay is never changed.
  • Bonuses and benefits on the check payslip — bonuses from the uploaded payslip (fuel, takings, regular), pension contributions, phone and laundry; the check gross pay now matches to the last koruna.
  • Recalculating closed months after a roster fix — the app finds the closed months a roster change affects and offers to recalculate them. The roster is never rewritten backwards: every day is calculated with the roster version that was valid on that date.
  • Month picker and a background-work indicator — switch months in the pay overview with a dropdown or arrows; when the app is calculating in the background you can see it in the bottom right.
  • Yearly public-holiday overview — which ones were driven and what they paid extra, and which fell on a roster rest day where no entitlement arises.
  • Driver card import — chip card data via a reader (since v1.2.13).
  • MPV Live / Operations — real-time operation of Semily rosters: currently running trips, trip detail (delay, occupancy), a vehicle-position map and live delays (since v1.2.14, read-only).
  • Entry validation — time and value checks so a typo never reaches the payroll.
  • Silent auto-update — new versions install themselves; config and data are kept.
  • No cloud — no account, no server, data only on the computer.

How it works in practice

A bus driver gets a payslip every month with a single number on it. Verifying that number means going through the rosters, adding up driving hours, waiting time, night and weekend bonuses and meal allowances — by the collective agreement, not by feel. BudLine Panel does exactly that: it keeps a record of what was actually driven, calculates what should arrive, and puts it next to what really did.

1. Logging a day

A day comes from two sources. The roster says what was supposed to be driven — trips, times, kilometres, breaks. The DZVA (the daily vehicle operation log) is the dispatcher's paper: document number, bus, tachograph and odometer readings. The app combines both and works out the rest, so you only enter what differs from the plan.

  • Swapping the bus mid-day — just say which route you switched at. Times and kilometres for both vehicles are derived from the roster, and when the counter readings are filled in, actual figures win over the plan.
  • Two shifts in one day — if the dispatcher issues a new DZVA for the afternoon, the day splits into two shifts. Pay is apportioned by driving time; the meal allowance stays whole, because the whole day was spent at work.
  • Extra or dropped trips — the difference in driving time, time at work and kilometres is calculated; duration and distance come from the timetable, nothing is guessed.

2. The calculation

On top of the records runs the payroll calculation per the collective agreement: base rate, performance component, waiting time, difficult conditions, night, weekend and holiday bonuses, and the meal allowance by shift length. The result is visible day by day and for the whole month.

  • Monthly pay overview — a day-by-day breakdown: hours worked, how much of that was driving, which bonuses, how many koruna. Months are switched with a picker at the top.
  • Reports — my own payslip and a shift sheet in the same shape as the employer's paper, so they can be compared line by line.
  • Payroll check — item by item against the uploaded payslip, including bonuses and benefits; anything that does not match shows up straight away.
  • Roster check — an indicative estimate of what a given roster will bring, before it is driven.

3. Checks and honest numbers

The app is not supposed to invent figures. When it cannot be sure, it says so and would rather show nothing than show something wrong.

  • An implausible reading is not used — if an odometer reading works out to 50,000 km in a shift, or far beyond the plan, the app treats it as a typo and stays with the plan. It does not pass a typo off as a detour.
  • An empty field says why — whether the value is missing from the DZVA, or only one of the two readings is filled in. A reversed counter (end lower than start) is marked red.
  • The payroll truth stays with the employer's payslip — the app is a checking aid, not a replacement for the payroll office, and it says so on its own outputs.
  • Month close — once a month is closed the figures are locked into a snapshot so they cannot drift. If a roster later turns out to have been different from a certain date, the app offers a recalculation and records why.

4. Data stays at home

  • Runs locally on Windows (and macOS), no cloud, no account, no telemetry. Data lives on the computer.
  • Backups with a single button, optionally encrypted with a password. A restore can first be tried as a dry run into an isolated copy — you see what the backup contains and live data is never touched.
  • Updates download and install themselves; data and settings are kept.
  • Pages regenerate themselves whenever the data changes — there is nothing to refresh by hand.

What the app does not do

It is not a payroll system and it does not replace the payroll office. It does not calculate other people, it sends nothing to the employer and it has no web version. It is one driver's tool for checking his own pay — and it is written that way.

Installation (beta)

🔒 The app is not publicly downloadable. You will not find an installer here on purpose — I send the link personally to whoever I give the app to. The version number on this page stays: it fills in automatically from the same place the installed app uses for its silent updates.

🔒 The installer is password-protected — I share the password personally; it is not and will not be on the web.

🍏 macOS: on first launch, right-click the app → Open (it is unsigned, it's our own app). If it says "damaged", run xattr -cr "/Applications/BudLine Panel.app" in Terminal.

🛡️ Windows may show a SmartScreen warning (unsigned beta installer). If you know the app and expect it from me, click "More info" → "Run anyway".

⚠️ Close the running app before installing.

📖 Development diary — BudLine Panel

  • v1.2.103 — the last batch from the audit: encrypted MPV password, messages without paths, backup verification, accessibility (4 Sep 2026) — With this release every finding of the independent audit is closed. The MPV password used to be stored in plain text next to the program (on a Mac it could not even be saved); it now lives in the data folder and on Windows it is encrypted and tied to your account — the old file moves itself on first start. Error messages state the type and reason, but no longer print file paths on disk, and a permission error no longer leaves a temporary file behind. An encrypted backup can be verified and dry-run with its password directly in the app (previously only through a test restore), and a backup containing shifts or a month close is marked as sensitive. Accessibility: drop-down lists and icon buttons have names for screen readers, the home page has a heading. For development: a second start on an occupied port ends with a clear error. No change in the calculations.
  • v1.2.102 — starting over an empty data folder, a silent error found by the install test (4 Sep 2026) — The last fix from the independent audit: when the app starts over an empty data folder (typically a Mac after an update), it now creates the missing base files itself and the pages Shift overview, Vehicles, Documents and the shift editor show up normally — previously they ended in a 404 because three pages were saved next to the program instead of in the data folder. The installation test of this fix also found two bugs that would otherwise stay hidden: in the windowless mode every request to the app failed because of a write to a non-existent console, and the error record then triggered a rebuild of all pages over and over (every two seconds). Both are fixed, and the app now records for every failed request where exactly it happened — without paths and without personal data. No change in the calculations.
  • v1.2.101 — home overview after month close, a shift without a rule at CZK 0, pages only for the app (4 Sep 2026) — Three fixes from the independent audit. The home overview after closing a month showed zeros: closing moves shifts to the archive and the overview no longer counted them — now driven shifts include archived ones and a closed month shows the figures from the locked closing, marked "closed". A shift in a year without tariffs (2027, say) had a base of CZK 0, yet the holiday supplement and meal allowance were still silently added to the total; such a shift is now CZK 0, marked ⛔ in the overview with a red box on top saying what to add. A month without shifts reports "empty" instead of "high confidence". Security: pages with wage figures and exports are served only to requests that really come from the app itself — until now that applied only to the API. The installation test also found that clicking "recalculate" during the automatic page refresh could end in an error; fixed. No amount for 2026 changes, proven by comparing the old and new calculation on every month of this year.
  • v1.2.100 — taxes know the period, average earnings say where they come from (4 Sep 2026) — The control calculation of tax and contributions now knows which month it is computing: every rate must have a documented validity for that month (validity range, year, or the date you verified it). A rate without it ends up as "waiting for review" and the message names the rate and the period to confirm — previously, in 2027 for example, the 2026 rates would have been used silently. Night, weekend and holiday supplements are calculated from the average earnings of the previous quarter, which the app takes from the uploaded payslip. For a month without a payslip yet the app used the overall average — and never said so. It is now shown as a warning next to the calculation; the amounts stay the same as before. The fix came out of an independent audit and is backed by an exact comparison of the old and new calculation: hundreds of tax combinations and every month of this year come out identical to the crown.
  • v1.2.99 — a split day was only half-kept in the database (4 Sep 2026) — A day split into two shifts because of a bus swap (same roster, second DZVA from dispatch) had only one of its two halves in the database: the database treated „same date + same roster" as the same record and silently overwrote the second shift on save. Two different work obstacles on one day ended the same way. The shifts file was fine all along, but the file-vs-database consistency check therefore reported a permanent difference, the app read data from the file instead of the database, and a restore from the database would have lost the second half. Each shift is now kept in the database under its own identifier, so the database matches the file one to one. On the first start after the update the database converts itself and fills in the missing halves of split days from the file — nothing to do by hand. Verified in the installed app: a planted old database with a missing half repaired itself at start-up, and a day split inside the app has both halves in the database. The bug was only caught by the previous version's test; it had been in the app since 1.2.83. The pay calculation is unchanged.
  • v1.2.98 — fixes from an independent audit: data writes and history (4 Sep 2026) — The app went through an independent audit that confirmed the pay calculation to the crown but found four serious flaws in how data is stored and protected. All four are fixed in this version. Two quick shift saves in a row no longer lose one of them — a double-click, or an import running at the same time as a manual entry, could silently drop a shift even though both reported "saved"; the whole write now runs under one lock (verified with eight simultaneous writes: eight saved, previously one or two). If the shifts file goes missing (disk clean-up, a sync tool, a bad restore) the app no longer shows an empty record and, above all, the next save no longer overwrites the database — it shows the data from the database, blocks writes until the file is restored, Health reports it, and the file is rebuilt from the database automatically at start-up or with the "Restore exports" button. An incomplete (truncated) roster export is rejected: a file without the Výkon…KčCelkem summary used to be accepted and overwrote the roster (24 → 21 activities, amount gone); it now comes back with an explanation and the roster stays untouched, and a same-date update also shows the difference in rows. Roster version history now remembers the individual rows — a change of time or stop with the same date and the same summary used to create no version at all, and the previous schedule was gone for good. Plus text fields (licence plate and others) are escaped in the pages and the licence plate is checked on entry. The pay calculation is untouched; every fix is covered by a test that failed on the previous version.
  • v1.2.97 — fix for driven kilometres on the home page (2 Sep 2026) — The „Driven kilometres" overview showed only the current month in its all-time total. Once a month is closed its shifts move to the archive — and those were exactly what the kilometre total lost. On real data that meant 3,791 km instead of 45,760 km and two buses instead of eight. It now counts every shift that was actually driven, including closed months; shifts still waiting for review are not counted. The per-bus breakdown also always matches the total now. On a day when the vehicle was swapped, the odometer readings differ from the shift's kilometres (by 3.4 km on 30 July) and that difference used to disappear silently — it now shows as its own „unassigned to a vehicle" line, so the figures never drift apart.
  • v1.2.94–1.2.96 — holiday in the overviews and diagnostics (21 Aug – 1 Sep 2026) — Holiday is finally visible in the calendar: it used to share the colour of a rostered day off, so in a month with leave you couldn't tell one from the other. It now has its own colour and marker. Holiday pay is calculated and shown in two places — as its own line on the control payslip and in the gross-pay preview on the home page; both use the same calculation (hours taken × average earnings for the previous quarter, §§ 351–356 of the Czech Labour Code), so the two figures agree. A month with leave therefore no longer looks poorer than the actual pay will be. If the hours taken aren't filled in, the app deliberately doesn't price the leave and says why — it never guesses. New diagnostics: the app keeps its own record of what went wrong (errors, crashes, slow operations) together with what happened just before, so a problem can be traced afterwards — you don't have to remember what you were doing. Repeats of the same error are merged into one record with an occurrence count. If a NAS is set up at home, the record is sent there, so even a problem that happens on a different computer can be fixed; away from the home network the records wait and are sent once you're back. Without that setup nothing is sent anywhere — an installation on someone else's computer runs purely locally and knows about no NAS at all. Passwords, tokens and personal data are masked as they are written, not just before sending, and no cloud service is used.

New lines: pension contributions and phone as deductions, laundry as an untaxed allowance, and a transfer to account line at the bottom. Recalculating months that are already closed: when it turns out a roster was different from a certain date, the app finds the closed months affected and offers to recalculate them. The roster itself is never rewritten backwards — each day is calculated with the roster version valid on that date; months that would need a roster "from the future" are skipped with the reason stated. The pay overview now has a month picker (a dropdown plus previous/next arrows; months with no shifts are marked), and when the app is working in the background an indicator in the bottom right shows what it is doing and for how long. The roster editor can save an edit as a new version valid from a new date — the original stays in the archive, so older months keep being calculated with it; if the validity date is left unchanged the app warns that it would overwrite that same version. Also: a yearly public-holiday overview (which ones were driven and what they paid extra, and which fell on a roster rest day where no entitlement arises), the shift sheet can be laid next to the employer's paper and compared day by day, and a closed month no longer shows zeros. macOS fix: on a Mac a manually edited roster or a changed rate never reached the calculation — the app saved them somewhere other than where the calculation read them.
