Finance — Ledger

📒Finance — Ledger

Personal accounting book — banks, brokers, crypto, gold, crowdfunding, pension, loans, invoices, taxes and a retirement plan in one app on my own PC. It fetches and posts statements by itself, recalculates its overviews by itself, and can explain where every number came from. No cloud, no signup, fully encrypted.

What it does

I built it because I got tired of switching between online banking, broker websites and Excel. The app handles 25 different sources — bank and payment accounts, brokers including an options broker, crypto exchanges and wallets, P2P platforms, precious metals and pension savings. Instead of logging in to every platform, one page shows assets, cash, investments, debts and net worth — and for every number the app can show which rows it came from.

It finds its own work. Documents that are not in the book yet it finds by itself in watched folders and in my mailbox (a small watcher on a Raspberry Pi pulls attachments from Gmail). What is unambiguous it posts; what is not, it hands to me with a plain sentence saying why it left it alone. Twice a day it recalculates overviews, aligns account names and currencies with the statements, applies an extra loan repayment to the principal and clears the queue of rows waiting for a decision. If I start it after a week, it catches up on missed runs right after start.

Nothing is estimated. An account balance is a documented figure from the statement, not a sum of movements. An exchange rate is the Czech National Bank rate for that date. Every tax rule carries its paragraph, official source and verification date — a historical transaction is judged by the law of its own year. Where a document is missing or the law gives no answer, the app refuses to produce a number and says why. Every important calculation has an independent “shadow” recomputation watching over it.

No cloud, no data leaving. The database is a single SQLite file on my Windows machine. A full backup carries the whole system (database, documents, settings) and, if it includes API keys, it is always encrypted (AES-256-GCM). The book travels between my PC and laptop over a home NAS with protection against overwriting a newer book with an older one. Updates arrive silently from the NAS and are verified by checksum.

Key features

The app has 14 modules in the main menu and around 150 screens. This is a selection of what I use most.

  • Overview — assets, cash, investments and net worth on one page; portfolio allocation with a “where and how much” breakdown on click, emergency-reserve and limit tiles, a freshness traffic light for statements (which account is missing a statement and since when), loan coverage by crypto value, cash-flow outlook and financial independence.
  • Imports — 25 sources without touching code — bank statements (PDF, CSV and API), payment accounts, XTB, eToro, Trading 212, Interactive Brokers, an options broker, crypto exchanges plus hardware and software wallets, P2P platforms, precious metals, pension savings, payslips. The wizard tells you exactly which export to download. A bank statement is verified against itself before posting (opening balance + movements = closing balance) and a statement that does not add up is rejected as a whole. The same file imported twice writes nothing.
  • Pairing centre — transfers between my own accounts, bank ↔ broker, bank ↔ crypto exchange, payment ↔ invoice, salary ↔ payslip, instalment ↔ loan. Documented pairs (variable symbol, counter-account, contract number) are matched automatically; a matching amount alone is never enough. Every leg of a transfer has a status or a written reason why it has no pair.
  • Invoices and recurring payments — received invoices with supplier and account, standing orders confirmed from history with one click, expected payments in the calendar and in the cash-flow outlook. A supplier can change its account and variable symbol and the app still knows it is the same company. QR payment of an invoice by phone (the app never pays anything itself).
  • Institutions and accounts — one directory of institutions, companies, my accounts and counterparty accounts. The book distinguishes my account, a counterparty account, a broker client number and a held position; it aligns account name and currency with statement headers. An institution detail shows accounts, cards, savings envelopes, loans and connection status.
  • Stocks and ETFs — positions and cash at all brokers, FIFO with corporate actions (split, merger, symbol change), dividends and withholding tax by country, performance, allocation, dividend calendar, goals, portfolio health. A ticker is not an identity — a security is recognised by ISIN.
  • Crypto — holdings by coin and wallet, lots and FIFO, own transfers between exchange and wallet linked into a chain (a transfer is not a new purchase), on-chain sync of a hardware wallet from its public key (addresses derived locally, balance matching the wallet's own app to the last digit), reconciliation where “the difference is zero or has a named cause”, a tax trail from disposal through lots to the paragraph, average purchase price, and how much would be left to repay a loan after actually selling crypto.
  • Loans — records from bank repayment schedules (matching the bank to the cent), extra repayments and early payoff from statements, “which loan to repay first” strategy, refinancing, and a break-even point for “repay or invest” instead of a verdict. A loan drawdown is not income; refinancing is neutral on both sides.
  • Taxes — a separate module with 15 sub-pages on a registry of 50 tax rules (each with paragraph, official source and verification date). The CZK 100,000 value test and the CZK 40 million cap on gross income, the three-year time test, dividends, options, crypto, P2P, wages. A package for the accountant as ZIP (XLSX + CSV + PDF) with a checksum — it is supporting material, not a tax return; an independent shadow engine recomputes every year.
  • Retirement and financial independence — pension savings from the documented annual statement plus contributions from payslips, insurance periods from the social security office (gaps are derived), pre-retirement outlook. Financial independence separates FIRE capital from net worth (reserve, pension and illiquid P2P are excluded), builds the target on actual essential spending, a seeded stochastic simulation, stress scenarios and a lever optimiser using the real projection.
  • Cash-flow outlook, reserve, goals, risks — 12 months ahead from the contractual calendar, recurring payments and documented balances (the accounting identity of every month is measured); reserve from regular spending of the last closed months; financial goals by priority; DSTI, DTI, liquidity and concentration risks — a missing input is “cannot compute”, never zero.
  • Gold and crowdfunding — Bullion coins with spot price and a documented price series from invoices, the Gold Account derived from bank payments and invoices, P2P platforms with statement value and a breakdown by investment.
  • Cash and audit — one ledger every view is built from; a check between each pair of documents “balance + movements = next balance”; the difference is stated as an amount, not a colour.
  • Saving without surprises — every form shows “Unsaved changes → Saving → Saved / Error”, leaving with unsaved changes asks first, saving starts a visible recalculation of dependent data with phases, and screens refresh without F5. Fixing an old row propagates by itself to every affected domain (impact graph, 13 domains in fixed order); the whole book is never recalculated needlessly.
  • System: health, engines, operations — “What is missing” with one of six verbs (Add, Sync, Import, Pair, Check, Fix); 20 application engines with a human-readable state and a link to where it gets resolved (an unmeasured engine never pretends to be fine); reconciliation that modules agree with each other to the cent; operation history and recovery of an unfinished recalculation; incident diagnostics with a problem fingerprint instead of a white screen.
  • Backup, transfer and uninstall — full backup of the whole system in parts with verification, API keys transferred only in an encrypted envelope, restore by chunked upload with preview, complete uninstall including data (external backups are never deleted). Silent updates from the home NAS with SHA-256 checksum.
  • Quick search Ctrl+K — finds 130+ pages without diacritics and even by path; destructive actions cannot even be written into the palette.

Status

Working v0.10.6 (September 2026). Local Windows app, ~9 months of active development, silent updates from the home NAS. Around 10,500 automated tests (Python + frontend + Playwright E2E over an isolated copy of the book) and an independent verifier for every larger task. Private — no public hosting, no third-party API.
