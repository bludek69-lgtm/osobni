What it does

Why it exists: phone control of a smart home is fast only relative to how slow it is. By the time you unlock the phone, find the app, tap the right icon, scroll — you could've just turned the lights on by hand. A wall panel solves this exactly. Always on, always ready, everything important on the first screen.

Hardware: Raspberry Pi 4 (small computer the size of a deck of cards) + 7" touch display, screwed to the hallway wall. About €100 total. Lives in a Chromium browser launched in „kiosk mode“ — no OS UI, no menu, just the full-window app.

What it does: control all lights, start/stop radio, switch scene (relax / cinema / cooking / dinner / arriving / leaving), adjust heating, see what the house is consuming in real time, who's home, who's asleep, where motion was last detected.

Scenes Loxone-style: big tiles, tap „Cinema“ with a thumb and the whole flat reconfigures — lights to 30%, music quietens, blind blocks the street, heating up one degree. One touch instead of ten.

Key features

  • Touchscreen in hallway — Raspberry Pi 4 + 7" touch on wall. Always on, always ready.
  • Scenes with one thumb — Relax, Cinema, Cooking, Dinner, Romance, Work, Arrive, Leave — one tap configures everything.
  • Light control — All rooms, dim, color, scenes. No phone hunting.
  • Radio audio — Start/stop internet radios, choose speaker, volume.
  • Heating & energy — A large Heating page: pick a room, tap Comfort / Night / Away / Schedule, or set the exact temperature with a rotary dial. Plus boiler runtime, TRV zones and current draw.
  • Camera and car watch — Live view from the camera outside, right on the panel. The system recognises on its own that someone is standing at the car and announces it out loud and to the phone — while ignoring people just crossing the yard.
  • No phone, no login — No sign-in needed, nothing to search for. Open the door — panel shows it.

📖 Development diary — Home wall panel

  • Safer link to the smart home (October 2026) — The panel talks to the smart home through its own intermediary that holds the access key. After a security review only the panel itself may use it — other devices on the network can neither control the house through it nor read its settings.
  • No secrets over the network (October 2026) — While reading the code I found that the panel's simple web server would hand over, to any other computer in the home network, files that do not belong there — including the smart-home access key and the camera login. Now it serves only the panel's own screens and refuses everything else; the key is kept outside the public files. Verified from another computer: the screens load, the sensitive files do not.
  • Night mode of the display (October 2026) — The panel in the hallway used to light up the room at night. In the evening it now dims, at night (or when the house is in sleep mode) the display switches off, and a touch lights it faintly for two minutes. It reads the state of the house from the same source as the dashboard; when that is unavailable it falls back to the clock, and when in doubt it stays at full brightness — a black panel by day is worse than a glowing one at night.
  • Heating on the panel (October 2026) — A separate large Heating page: pick a room, tap Comfort, Night, Away or Schedule, or set the exact temperature with a rotary dial. A manual change holds until the next change in the schedule, then the head returns to the plan on its own. The panel and the Config Center PC app share the same control module.
  • Touch panel on the wall — Raspberry Pi 4 + 7-inch touch display in the hallway, always on and ready. Scenes with one thumb (Relax, Cinema, Cooking, Dinner, Romance, Work, Arrive, Leave).
  • Control of the whole flat — Lights (all rooms, dim, color, scenes), audio from internet radios (speaker choice, volume), heating and energy (boiler runtime, TRV zones, current draw).
  • No phone, no login — Nobody has to sign in or search for anything; you open the door and the panel shows it.
  • Camera in front of the house (August 2026) — The panel shows a live image from the camera and the latest events with previews. You don't have to go anywhere or look for an app on the phone.
  • Car watch (August 2026) — The system recognises on its own that someone is standing at the car and announces it out loud in the kitchen and with a message to the phone. Recognising a person was not the hard part, but telling “someone is at my car” from “someone is walking across the yard” — the yard is a thoroughfare and over a hundred people pass through daily. The first attempt even made it worse: false alarms increased. It turned out why — on a flat image a person fifteen metres further away can appear right next to the car. Now it judges how large the person is in the frame: someone standing at the car looks big, someone far away looks small. False alarms dropped from twenty-six to zero.
  • Two alert volumes — Out loud when someone is right at the car. A quieter message to the phone when someone is only lingering near it. So that nobody starts switching the alerts off after a week.
  • Reliable 24/7 — Runs all the time, restarts itself after a power cut, about a year without problems.

Note: the app runs locally only; no real data or personal information goes to the web.
