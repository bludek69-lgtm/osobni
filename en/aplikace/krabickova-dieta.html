🥗Meal Planner

My own meal planner — instead of signing up to diet apps that track me, I have my own that runs only in my browser. Generates a weekly meal-box plan based on my calorie target, computes a shopping list, tracks macros. No account, no cloud.

React TypeScript Vite PWA Tailwind

← Back to My apps

What it does

Why it exists: I tried several mainstream diet apps (MyFitnessPal, Yazio, Lifesum…) and they all annoyed me. Either they wanted an account and pushed ads, or didn't know Czech meals, or forced a specific nutrition ideology. This one runs only in my browser — profile lives in localStorage, no server sees my weight, calories, or what I'm eating today.

How it works: I enter my height, weight, age, activity. The app computes my daily calorie need (Mifflin-St Jeor for resting metabolism + activity multiplier). For weight loss it recommends an asymmetric deficit. For muscle gain it also prioritizes higher-protein recipes.

Weekly meal-box plan: The app searches a database of ~21 recipes and builds 7 days × 5 meals (breakfast, snack, lunch, snack, dinner) so the daily kcal lands within ±10% of target. Then it generates a shopping list — sums all ingredients for the week and groups them by category. Export to CSV or print.

Optional Rohlík: If I'm a Rohlík customer, I can have an agent (Claude) prepare a cart proposal from the weekly plan, paste JSON into Settings and see the cart preview with totals vs minimum vs delivery price. The order is always confirmed manually in Rohlík UI — the app physically cannot order or pay (5-layer defense-in-depth).

PWA = works offline: After first load it caches and works without internet. Installable as a desktop app on Windows via Edge. No app store updates, no notifications, no telemetry.

Key features

  • 0.9.17 beta — maintenance release (August 2026) — no new feature and no fix. The only thing that changed is a developer-side limit on package size, which never makes it into the installation at all. Released to verify that the whole build and distribution chain works with the new limit. If you are wondering why the app updated itself and you cannot see anything new — that is the reason.
  • 0.9.16 beta — data from a watch and a phone (August 2026) — I export my data from Apple Health (export.xml) or Health Connect (JSON) and upload it in the Fitness settings. The app reads steps, active energy, heart rate, sleep and workouts from it. When there is a measured active energy for today, it shows that instead of its own estimate and notes how far the estimate was off — an estimate never overwrites a measurement, not even when it is higher. Importing the same file again does not duplicate anything. A direct connection to HealthKit or Health Connect is not possible: those are native phone interfaces and Meal Planner runs on Windows, so importing a file is the only route that exists. Health data stays on the computer. 1077 unit tests, e2e 73/73.
  • 0.9.15 beta — the Fitness module (August 2026) — training and food finally in one place. I plan a workout by hand, or have a whole week suggested from my goal, equipment, time and what hurts; for every suggestion I can see why it looks the way it does, and the same input always produces the same plan. I log the sets I did, a daily check tells the app how I feel, and on a bad day it suggests doing less or taking a rest — nobody blames me for a skipped workout. The workout appears in the same calendar as the meals, and optionally I can have more energy on a training day without changing the weekly intake (protein holds its daily target). Before the first plan there is a short readiness questionnaire; if something comes out of it that an app should not be deciding, it builds no plan and says why. With pregnancy, heart and vascular conditions, rehabilitation, an eating disorder, for children and with a doctor's restriction it does not plan at all — the rules for those situations have to be approved by a professional first. The content of the 17 exercises has not yet been reviewed by a trainer or a physiotherapist, it was written by the developer, and the app says so out loud on every exercise. 1050 unit tests, e2e 70/70.
  • 0.9.14 beta — safer data and a reliable backup restore (August 2026) — only the app itself may now write into its local database; another program on the computer cannot slip anything in, even if it hits the right address. At startup it also verifies that what is running on its port really is its own helper server — if not, the window does not open at all and leaves an explanation behind. An unfinished restore now completes itself on the next start (before, it could hang and block data from loading), undoing a restore cleans up after itself, and deleted items do not come back even after clearing browser data. Settings gained a Restore and sync diagnostics card — on a problem it shows an understandable reason and a code to report, and passwords never get into it. 820 unit tests, e2e 43/43, install test with the window open.
  • 0.9.13 beta — a big reliability wave after a full audit (July 2026) — my own recipes can finally be scheduled by hand onto a specific day and meal (with search and a choice of portions) and they sit in one catalog with the built-in ones, so they are counted the same way everywhere. A full backup and restore of all data was added (recipes, meal plan, boxes, shopping, fridge, settings) — and after a restore the recipes appear immediately, with no restart. Deleting a recipe that is used in the meal plan no longer breaks the Dashboard or Shopping — the app warns in advance. The shopping list correctly merges the same ingredient written in different ways ("Tomato" and "tomatoes"). On the desktop, updates, mirroring into the database and live Rohlík prices work again.
  • 0.9.12 beta — more reliable auto-update (July 2026) — the app now updates itself reliably even with the window open: it waits until it fully closes and releases its files, and retries once if the install doesn't go through the first time. Earlier (0.9.11) added a loop guard — if an auto-update fails, instead of retrying forever it offers a manual installer download. Personal tracking and data unchanged. 605 unit tests, verified end-to-end.
  • 0.9.10 beta — Tracking whether I follow the plan (July 2026) — for each meal in the box view I mark eaten / swapped / skipped in one click. The overview then shows a weekly summary (how many meals on plan, how many full days). Finally I can see whether I actually follow the plan, not just make it — and connect it to weight trends. Personal tracking, no judgment. 605 unit tests, e2e 27/27.
  • 0.9.9 beta — Pantry & a more robust app (July 2026) — Pantry: in Shopping I mark what I always have at home (salt, oil, spices…) and the app stops adding it to the weekly list or the Rohlík cart — no more buying salt over and over. If a page hits an error, instead of a blank screen you get a clear message with a recovery button (your data stays intact). Faster first load (large pages load on open). Under the hood: removed ~500 lines of unused legacy code, 594 unit tests, e2e 27/27.
  • 0.9.8 beta — new navigation, smarter shopping & Trust Center (July 2026) — unified cockpit navigation plus: recipe import from text, shopping grouped by store aisle, a week flow on the overview (Plan → Fridge → Shop → Prep → Eat today), a Trust Center (the app clearly states what is a live price vs estimate, verified vs internal macros), and persistent ingredient → Rohlík product mapping (never orders by itself). Under the hood: 597 unit tests, fixed e2e suite (27 passing). Beta / NOT_FINAL — shipped via auto-update, full re-install verification still pending.
  • Auto-generated weekly plan — Combo search for 7×5 meals, daily kcal within ±10% target. Repeat-recipe penalty for variety.
  • BMR/TDEE calculator — Mifflin-St Jeor formula + activity multiplier. Asymmetric deficit/surplus by weekly weight change.
  • Recommended target banner — When metrics shift the recommended kcal, app offers „Use recommended“ (never a silent change).
  • Weight tracking — Own SVG chart without external library, history in localStorage. No medical claims.
  • Loss/gain optimizer — Weight loss → prefers higher-protein recipes. Muscle gain → same plus tighter kcal target.
  • Rohlík integration (optional) — Agent prepares cart JSON, you see total vs minimum vs delivery price. Order always confirmed manually.
  • Shopping list with export — Aggregates ingredients from weekly plan, groups by category, CSV or print.
  • PWA & privacy — Installable as a desktop app on Windows (installer), runs fully locally with no cloud. No account, no telemetry.
